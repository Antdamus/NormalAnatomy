CORE_SOURCE_BASIS: Core Radiology, Second Edition, Gastrointestinal Imaging - Liver; relevant retrieved coverage includes GI pages 105-115, 119-122, and cholangiocarcinoma coverage on GI page 146 from junzi-shi-core-radiology-a-visual-approach-to.pdf.
CORE_FACTS_USED:

* FNH: arterial hyperenhancement, later isoattenuation/isointensity, T2-hyperintense central scar, delayed scar enhancement with extracellular contrast, hepatobiliary contrast retention by the lesion but not the scar, spoke-wheel Doppler pattern, and sulfur-colloid/HIDA behavior.
* Cavernous hemangioma: peripheral discontinuous blood-pool nodular enhancement with progressive centripetal fill-in, T2 hyperintensity, and possible central nonenhancing degeneration in giant lesions.
* Hepatic adenoma: arterial hyperenhancement, later iso-/hypoenhancement, fat/hemorrhage-related heterogeneity, out-of-phase signal loss when intracellular lipid is present, lack of bile ducts/HIDA uptake, and the >5 cm resection threshold.
* Conventional HCC: arterial enhancement, nonperipheral washout, capsule/pseudocapsule, mosaic architecture, fat/blood products, and tumor-in-vein as an invasive feature.
* Fibrolamellar HCC: young noncirrhotic patient profile, large heterogeneous mass, fibrotic T1/T2-hypointense central scar, and possible capsular retraction.
* Metastases: most are hypovascular and best seen on portal venous phase; MRI is generally T1 hypointense/T2 hyperintense; mucinous metastases may calcify.
* Epithelioid hemangioendothelioma: multiple peripheral/subcapsular masses, target/halo appearance, T1-low/T2-high signal, coalescence, and capsular retraction.
* Mass-forming cholangiocarcinoma: delayed/progressive enhancement, peripheral biliary dilatation, capsular retraction, and obstructive/atrophic changes.
* Budd-Chiari: hepatic venous outflow obstruction with chronic caudate hypertrophy, peripheral atrophy, and prominent regenerative nodules; acute disease may show mottled liver and flip-flop enhancement.
