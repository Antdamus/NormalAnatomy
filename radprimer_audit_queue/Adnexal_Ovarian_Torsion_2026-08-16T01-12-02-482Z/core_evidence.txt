CORE_FACTS_USED:

* Adnexal torsion results from twisting of the ovarian vascular pedicle with potential vascular compromise.
* Torsion occurs mainly in reproductive-age women and commonly occurs in pregnancy.
* A lead-point adnexal mass predisposes to torsion; Core emphasizes ovarian dermoid.
* Dual ovarian arterial supply explains why color Doppler flow may remain detectable despite torsion.
* Classic ultrasound pattern includes an enlarged edematous ovary, abnormal Doppler findings, and free fluid.
* Ovary >4 cm, unusual ovarian position, peripheral follicles, and variable Doppler findings are key imaging clues.
* Lack of flow is not required and, when present, usually indicates necrosis or infarction.
* Normal/intermittent flow and absent or reversed arterial diastolic flow may occur.
* CT and MRI share the core morphology of ovarian enlargement and peripheral follicles from central hemorrhage/edema.
* Twisting of the ovarian pedicle is uncommon to visualize but pathognomonic when identified.
