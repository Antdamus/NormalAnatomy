🧭 Focal Hypervascular Liver Lesion

Source Basis: Core + RadPrimer + STATdx synthesis. Core was used only for the retrieved liver-mass sections documented in the audit block; RadPrimer supplies the differential backbone and STATdx supplies the selected image/caption set.
🔍 Summary
  • Characterize both morphology and hemodynamics across phases; the key discriminator is how a lesion behaves relative to liver and blood pool.
  • Hemangioma: blood-pool-equivalent enhancement with peripheral discontinuous nodules and progressive centripetal fill-in; small lesions may flash fill.
  • FNH: bright homogeneous arterial enhancement with near-isodensity/isointensity on portal and delayed phases; a central scar may be present.
  • Arterioportal shunts: commonly small and peripheral in cirrhotic liver, often wedge-shaped, arterial-only, and associated with early portal-branch filling.
  • THAD: peripheral wedge-shaped arterial hyperperfusion that normalizes on portal venous imaging, usually from altered portal flow.
  • HCC: typically heterogeneous arterial hyperenhancement with portal/delayed washout; evaluate for venous and biliary invasion.
  • Hypervascular metastases: multiplicity, heterogeneity, and ring enhancement favor malignancy; common primaries include neuroendocrine tumors, melanoma, and renal cell carcinoma.
  • Hepatic adenoma: usually heterogeneously hypervascular and may contain fat, necrosis, or hemorrhage; gadoxetate-delayed relative hypointensity is illustrated in the supplied inflammatory adenoma case.
  • HHT: enlarged hepatic arteries and draining veins with heterogeneous arterial-phase enhancement and early venous opacification.
  • Large regenerative nodules in Budd-Chiari can be multiple and hypervascular, with persistent portal-phase enhancement in the supplied CT/MR examples.
  • Fibrolamellar HCC: large heterogeneous mass in a young patient, often with a large central scar that may calcify.
  • Peripheral cholangiocarcinoma: may be hypervascular but delayed persistent enhancement and capsular retraction favor it over HCC.
  • Hepatic angiomyolipoma: nonfatty tissue may be hypervascular; macroscopic fat is visible in only about half of hepatic AMLs in the article.
  • Hepatic angiosarcoma can show bizarre eccentric hypervascular enhancement and multifocal liver/spleen involvement.
  • SVC obstruction can create left-lobe pseudolesional hyperenhancement through collateral pathways without a true mass.
  • Peliosis can show early globular or ring-like vascular enhancement with progressive fill-in, overlapping with hemangioma.
⚠️ Pearls / Pitfalls
  • Do not equate every arterial focus in cirrhosis with HCC; transient wedge-shaped shunts that disappear on later phases are an important mimic.
  • Do not equate every central scar with FNH; fibrolamellar HCC is typically large and heterogeneous, and calcification within the scar is a major warning feature.
  • Hemangioma should track blood pool; persistent delayed enhancement denser than liver and blood vessels is not the expected hemangioma pattern.
⭐ Super Summary
Read a hypervascular liver lesion by phase behavior first, then morphology, background liver, multiplicity, vascular invasion, scar/fat/necrosis, and collateral or portal-flow clues.
