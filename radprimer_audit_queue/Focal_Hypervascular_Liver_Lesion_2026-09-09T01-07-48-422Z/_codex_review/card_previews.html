How does increasing neovascularity during hepatocarcinogenesis explain arterial hyperenhancement?

Progression from regenerative nodule to dysplastic nodule to HCC is accompanied by increasing neovascularity. Increasing arterial tumor supply produces arterial hyperenhancement. Portal/delayed relative washout and an enhancing capsule are associated imaging findings, assessed separately.

Note 28

XJSFJM6QX3YN

Why does a THAD appear as arterial-phase hyperenhancement that disappears on portal venous imaging?

A THAD is a perfusion phenomenon caused by reduced portal venous inflow to a territory, commonly from obstruction or compression of a portal vein branch. Compensatory increased arterial flow makes the involved wedge of liver transiently hyperenhancing on arterial phase. Once portal venous enhancement equilibrates, the area becomes isodense to background liver; the key interpretation pivot is that there is no true persistent mass.

FVUDDPSUC5EG

What explains typical FNH uptake of gadoxetate on hepatobiliary-phase MRI?

FNH contains functioning hepatocytes and usually retains gadoxetate, giving iso- or hyperintensity relative to liver on hepatobiliary-phase imaging.
Outside clarification (Sciarra et al., 2019): Hepatocyte uptake transporters, including OATP1B3, explain uptake; bile ductules alone are not the uptake mechanism. Transporter expression can be retained in some adenomas, particularly beta-catenin-activated lesions, so hepatobiliary brightness is not exclusive to FNH.

How can SVC obstruction create focal arterial hyperenhancement in the left hepatic lobe without a true mass?

With SVC obstruction, collateral venous pathways can route contrast-rich blood from thoracoabdominal collaterals toward the portal venous system, including pathways involving superficial epigastric/umbilical or internal mammary/inferior phrenic veins. This produces early focal enhancement, classically in the left lobe/medial segment, without mass effect. The imaging pivot is to trace the collateral pathway and confirm venous-phase normalization rather than diagnosing a hepatic tumor.
