CORE_FACTS_USED:

* HCC develops through regenerative nodule -> dysplastic nodule -> HCC with increasing neovascularity; classic HCC shows arterial enhancement, portal/delayed washout, enhancing capsule, and may restrict diffusion or be mildly T2 hyperintense.
* HCC commonly invades portal/hepatic veins, IVC, and bile ducts; enhancement or Doppler flow within venous thrombus suggests tumor in vein.
* Fibrolamellar HCC occurs in young patients without cirrhosis and is typically a large heterogeneous mass with a fibrotic central scar; the scar is T1/T2 hypointense.
* Mass-forming cholangiocarcinoma demonstrates delayed/progressive enhancement.
* Hypervascular metastases include melanoma, carcinoid/choriocarcinoma, renal cell carcinoma, and thyroid primaries.
* Cavernous hemangioma shows blood-pool-matched peripheral discontinuous nodular enhancement with progressive centripetal fill-in; MRI is typically T2 hyperintense.
* Classic hemangioma ultrasound appearance is a circumscribed homogeneous echogenic mass with no color-Doppler flow; posterior acoustic enhancement may occur.
* FNH is a stealth lesion outside the arterial phase; it shows homogeneous arterial enhancement and typically retains gadoxetate on hepatobiliary phase because hepatocytes and biliary ductules are present.
* Hepatic adenoma lacks bile ducts, is typically arterial hyperenhancing with later iso-/hypoenhancement, may contain fat or hemorrhage, is usually photopenic on sulfur colloid, and generally lacks HIDA uptake.
* Core states incidentally discovered hepatic adenomas >5 cm are usually resected because of hemorrhage risk.
* Chronic Budd-Chiari can produce prominent regenerative nodules and collateral/venous-outflow abnormalities that affect interpretation of hypervascular liver nodules.
