CORE_FACTS_USED:

* HCC progression from regenerative nodule to dysplastic nodule to HCC, with increasing neovascularity; classic arterial enhancement, portal venous/delayed washout, capsule, T2 hyperintensity, possible diffusion restriction, and tumor-in-vein assessment.
* Hepatic adenoma has a relatively high hemorrhage risk; arterial hyperenhancement with iso-/hypoenhancement on portal venous/delayed phases; variable ultrasound appearance; usually photopenic sulfur-colloid appearance and paucity of HIDA uptake; adenomas >5 cm are usually resected.
* Simple hepatic cysts are thin-walled and nonenhancing; ultrasound is anechoic with posterior enhancement and no internal Doppler flow; CT attenuation is 0-20 HU; MRI is low T1/high T2. ADPLD produces innumerable nonenhancing simple hepatic cysts.
* Most hepatic metastases are hypovascular and best appreciated on portal venous CT; metastases are generally T1 hypointense/T2 hyperintense, while blood products or melanin may be T1 hyperintense; ultrasound may show a target sign.
* Core identifies adenoma, HCC, and metastases among hemorrhagic hepatic lesion patterns.
* Core liver-trauma coverage supplies the CT-based AAST liver injury framework, including subcapsular hematoma, laceration depth, vascular injury/active bleeding, intraperitoneal extension, and major hepatic venous injury.
