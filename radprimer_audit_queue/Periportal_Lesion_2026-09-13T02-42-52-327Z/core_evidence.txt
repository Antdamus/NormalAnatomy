CORE_FACTS_USED:

* Viral hepatitis: when imaging is abnormal, periportal edema may produce a starry-sky ultrasound appearance and fluid on both sides of portal veins on CT/MRI; gallbladder-wall edema may coexist.
* Portal-vein thrombosis: lack of portal flow/echogenic thrombus; long-standing thrombosis produces multiple periportal collateral vessels (cavernous transformation).
* Pneumobilia is central branching gas; portal venous gas extends peripherally; both can be echogenic with dirty shadowing on ultrasound.
* Ascending cholangitis: bile-duct wall thickening/hyperenhancement with intraluminal debris and/or choledocholithiasis when abnormal.
* PSC: beaded irregular intra- and extrahepatic ducts; AIDS cholangiopathy can mimic PSC and more often includes papillary stenosis.
* Recent liver transplant: periportal edema can be a normal postoperative finding.
* Hepatic lymphoma can be infiltrative while preserving vasculature; it tends to be hypoechoic on ultrasound and hypoenhancing on CT/MRI.
* Cardiac hepatopathy: enlarged hepatic veins/IVC with reflux and mottled enhancement support passive congestion.
CORE_DERIVED_CARDS:
* High-Yield cards on acute viral hepatitis US and CT/MRI patterns.
* Boards Trap card separating pneumobilia from portal venous gas.
* UNKNOWN/support reasoning for cavernous transformation, passive congestion, hepatic lymphoma, PSC, ascending cholangitis, and post-transplant edema.
* High-Yield and Boards Trap cards on ascending cholangitis, post-transplant periportal edema, and periportal edema versus ductal dilation.
