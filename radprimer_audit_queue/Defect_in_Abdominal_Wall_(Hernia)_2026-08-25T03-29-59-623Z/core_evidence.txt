CORE_FACTS_USED:

* External hernias are the second most common cause of small bowel obstruction; approximately 75% occur in the groin, with most being inguinal.
* Indirect inguinal hernia neck is lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels; direct inguinal hernia neck is medial and protrudes through Hesselbach triangle.
* Incarceration means a hernia cannot be reduced; strangulation means ischemia from vascular compromise.
* Obturator hernia places bowel between the pectineus and obturator muscles.
* Femoral hernia enters the femoral canal below the inguinal ligament and tends to compress the adjacent femoral vein.
* Spigelian hernia occurs at the semilunar line.
* Richter hernia involves only the antimesenteric portion of bowel wall and is more likely to strangulate than obstruct.
