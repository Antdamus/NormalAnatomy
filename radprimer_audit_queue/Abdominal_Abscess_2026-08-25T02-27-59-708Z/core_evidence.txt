CORE_FACTS_USED:

* Hepatic pyogenic abscess may arise from bowel processes including diverticulitis, appendicitis, Crohn disease, and bowel surgery; E. coli is the most common causative organism in that Core section.
* Hepatic abscess may mimic metastasis as a ring-enhancing mass; MRI may show central T2 hyperintensity and restricted diffusion; ultrasound can evolve from ill-defined phlegmon to a well-defined hypoechoic collection with internal echoes.
* Appendicitis can progress to gangrenous/perforated appendicitis with abscess; periappendicular abscess is a secondary ultrasound finding.
* Crohn disease may be complicated by abscess and fistula formation.
* Diverticulitis is complicated when abscess is present, and abscesses may undergo percutaneous drainage.
* Pancreatitis-related collections may become infected; gas suggests infection but absence of gas does not exclude it.
* Splenic bacterial abscesses may have an irregular enhancing wall; gas is highly specific when present. Fungal splenic abscesses are typically small, multiple, and occur in immunocompromised patients.
* Core IR material was used to cross-check drainage-related concepts and catheter management.
