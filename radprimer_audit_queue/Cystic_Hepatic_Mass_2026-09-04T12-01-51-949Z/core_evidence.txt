CORE_FACTS_USED:

* Simple hepatic cyst: round/oval, thin or nearly imperceptible wall; US anechoic with posterior enhancement and no internal Doppler flow; CT 0–20 HU without enhancement; MRI low T1/high T2 without enhancement.
* Biliary hamartomas are small benign cystic lesions that do not communicate with the biliary tree; they are smaller/more irregular than simple cysts and may be echogenic when small. ADPLD produces innumerable nonenhancing simple cysts, and Core states that 40% of ADPKD patients have associated liver disease.
* Pyogenic abscess may mimic metastasis with ring enhancement; Core describes central T2 hyperintensity/restricted diffusion and the ultrasound evolution from altered echogenicity to a hypoechoic lesion with internal echoes. Hepatic fungal infection produces punctate microabscesses, and candidiasis commonly seeds liver and spleen in immunocompromised patients.
* Hydatid disease: floating membrane/daughter cysts, possible peripheral calcification, peripheral daughter cysts on ultrasound, water-lily sign, and hydatid sand. Amebic abscess is imaging-indistinguishable from pyogenic abscess; Core states pain occurs in 99% and the right hepatic dome is the most common location.
* Core explicitly lists ovarian cystadenocarcinoma and gastrointestinal sarcoma as causes of cystic hepatic metastases.
* Caroli disease is type V choledochal cyst disease with saccular intrahepatic bile-duct dilatation; the central-dot sign reflects portal/hepatic arterial branches within the dilated ducts. Choledochal cysts communicate with the biliary tree and carry cholangiocarcinoma risk stated as high as 25%.
* In liver transplantation, hepatic artery thrombosis is the most common vascular complication; biliary leaks are recognized transplant complications. Core also notes that suspected traumatic biliary injury may require HIDA to demonstrate active bile leak, with hepatobiliary-agent MRI as another option.
