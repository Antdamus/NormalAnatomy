CORE_FACTS_USED:

* Hepatic steatosis may be diffuse, focal, geographic, or nodular; focal fat lacks mass effect and characteristically permits vessels to traverse it.
* Steatosis shows increased hepatic echogenicity on ultrasound and opposed-phase signal loss on MRI; Core explains the fat-water cancellation mechanism.
* Core gives attenuation criteria of at least 10 HU below spleen on unenhanced CT and at least 25 HU below spleen on portal-venous-phase CT, while noting lower reliability after contrast.
* Viral hepatitis is commonly normal on imaging; when abnormal, periportal edema and gallbladder wall edema are characteristic.
* Most hepatic metastases are hypovascular and best seen on portal venous phase; MRI and ultrasound appearances and the melanoma exception were used.
* Hepatic lymphoma may be infiltrative, hypoenhancing, diffusion-restricting, hypoechoic, and associated with splenomegaly/lymphadenopathy.
* HCC arterial enhancement, washout, local vascular invasion, and tumor-in-vein criteria supported the infiltrative-HCC discriminator and vascular report-pivot card.
* Budd-Chiari findings include absent hepatic venous flow/contrast, hepatic-vein or IVC thrombus, collateralization, caudate sparing/hypertrophy, and peripheral hepatic injury.
* Cardiac hepatopathy/passive congestion findings include enlarged hepatic veins/IVC, refluxed contrast, mottled “nutmeg” enhancement, hepatomegaly, and ascites.
* Core hepatic fungal infection findings supported the microabscess appearance card.
