Clinical_Context: Q7M2X9L4V6KP
Entity_Label: Hepatic steatosis
Mechanism_Q: Why does hepatic steatosis lose signal on opposed-phase GRE MRI?
Mechanism: Normal liver voxels may contain both water and fat protons. On in-phase images their signals add; on opposed-phase images the fat and water components are out of phase and partially cancel, so voxels containing intracellular fat lose signal. This directly explains why opposed-phase signal dropout confirms hepatic steatosis.

Clinical_Context: L4V8Q2M7T9XR
Entity_Label: Budd-Chiari syndrome
Mechanism_Q: Why is the caudate lobe relatively spared and later hypertrophied in Budd-Chiari syndrome?
Mechanism: The caudate lobe has direct venous drainage into the IVC rather than relying on the main hepatic veins. Hepatic venous outflow obstruction therefore affects the peripheral liver more severely, while the caudate can remain relatively preserved and undergo compensatory hypertrophy. Imaging consequence: caudate enlargement with peripheral hepatic atrophy/injury supports chronic Budd-Chiari.
