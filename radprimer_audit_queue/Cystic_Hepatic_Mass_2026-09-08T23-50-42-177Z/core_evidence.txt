CORE_FACTS_USED:

* Simple hepatic cyst: thin/nearly imperceptible wall; CT 0-20 HU without enhancement; MRI low T1/high T2 without enhancement; ultrasound anechoic with posterior enhancement and no internal Doppler flow.
* Biliary hamartomas are small benign cystic lesions caused by abnormal bile-duct development, do not communicate with the biliary tree, and may appear echogenic on ultrasound.
* ADPLD produces innumerable nonenhancing hepatic cysts; 40% of patients with ADPKD have the hepatic disease process.
* Pyogenic abscess may ring-enhance, show central T2 hyperintensity/restricted diffusion, and evolve to a hypoechoic lesion with internal echoes on ultrasound.
* Hepatic fungal infection produces tiny hypoenhancing microabscesses; lesions may be T2 hyperintense/restrict diffusion and appear hypoechoic on ultrasound.
* Hydatid cyst may contain daughter cysts or a floating membrane; ultrasound may show daughter cysts, hydatid sand, and the water-lily sign.
* Amebic abscess may be indistinguishable from pyogenic abscess on imaging.
* Biliary cystadenoma/cystadenocarcinoma is typically multiseptated; mural nodules increase concern for malignant transformation.
* Caroli disease is saccular intrahepatic bile-duct dilatation with biliary communication; portal-tract branches create the central-dot sign.
* Cystic hepatic metastases include ovarian cystadenocarcinoma and gastrointestinal sarcoma; focal fat is distinguished by vessels traversing the region and opposed-phase signal loss.
* Hepatic artery thrombosis is the most common vascular complication of liver transplantation, supporting the transplant-biloma reporting pivot.
