=== ESSENTIAL INFORMATION ===
Scope and organizing distinction
- [Synthesis of Both] The RadPrimer topic includes focal periportal lesions, ductal disease, vascular structures, gas, cysts, fibrosis and calcification. The manually paired STATdx article concentrates on periportal lucency/edema and its mimics. Preserve the broader RadPrimer scope; periportal lesion and periportal edema are not interchangeable labels.
- [STATdx] Increased hepatic extracellular-fluid production or impaired lymphatic clearance distends loose areolar tissue in the portal triads. The tracking fluid may be lymph, bile or blood.
- [STATdx] Periportal edema surrounds the portal triad, whereas a dilated bile duct lies along one side of its accompanying portal vein. Obstruction may also produce edema, so both findings can coexist. Clotted portal branches can mimic nonenhancing dilated ducts.
- [STATdx] MRCP and direct cholangiography demonstrate ductal anatomy; the article names MRCP/ERCP as tools for distinguishing branching nonenhancing structures. Preserve this source comparison without inventing a management pathway or recommending invasive testing solely for this sign.
- [Both] US/Doppler distinguishes echogenic material, cystic structures and flowing vessels; CT/MR add the surrounding distribution, enhancement and ductal relationships. Match the finding to the source modality and case context.

Helpful Clues for Common Diagnoses — RadPrimer order

1. Ascending Cholangitis
- [RadPrimer] Periductal edema/inflammation produces hypo- or hyperechogenicity near dilated intrahepatic ducts. Look for circumferential duct-wall thickening, echogenic purulent bile/sludge and an obstructing common-duct stone. RadPrimer image 1 directly demonstrates thickened common-bile-duct walls and intraluminal debris on color Doppler US.
- [STATdx] Ascending infection may follow common-duct stone passage or other sphincter of Oddi incompetence allowing reflux of duodenal contents. T1/T2 MR can show thickened duct walls with progressive wall hyperenhancement on contrast-enhanced T1 imaging.
- [STATdx] Periportal edema, duct dilation and hepatic abscesses can coexist. STATdx images 10 and 11 preserve the CT/direct-cholangiography relationship between abscess cavities and pus-filled ducts. STATdx images 12 and 13 preserve the post-Whipple context, periportal edema, mildly dilated ducts, pneumobilia and liver abscess.
- [Curation] CT/cholangiography supply complication and procedure detail; they do not replace the ultrasound wall/debris example.

2. Cavernous Transformation of Portal Vein
- [RadPrimer] Subacute or chronic portal-vein obstruction generates serpiginous collateral channels along the expected portal-vein course. Doppler demonstrates hepatopetal collateral flow. The occluded vein may be enlarged acutely, small or imperceptible chronically, and lack Doppler flow.
- [RadPrimer] RadPrimer image 2 shows tortuous collateralized flow in chronic thrombosis. Its mixed Doppler colors reflect differently oriented vessel segments relative to the transducer; retain that caption explanation with the image.
- [STATdx] Cavernous channels may resemble edema on unenhanced CT but enhance like vessels on CECT. Nonenhancing thrombosed portal branches can resemble ducts. STATdx images 28 and 29 show occluded portal branches and collaterals in a hypercoagulable patient after autologous bone marrow transplantation.
- [Curation] The CT pair supplies related thrombosis/collateral anatomy, but does not supply the Doppler flow-direction demonstration.

3. Portosystemic Collaterals
- [RadPrimer] Serpiginous hypoechoic channels occur within or near the portal triad with cirrhosis, portal hypertension or portal thrombosis. The source includes intrahepatic portal-to-portal, portal-to-hepatic and portal-to-systemic channels; paraumbilical recanalization; gastroesophageal channels; and lienorenal, mesenteric and retroperitoneal routes.
- [RadPrimer] Color Doppler maps their extent and the source's hepatofugal-flow distinction from cavernous transformation. RadPrimer image 3 shows a right portal-to-right hepatic venous shunt. RadPrimer image 4 follows a recanalized paraumbilical vein from the left portal vein along the falciform ligament toward the inferior epigastric vein.
- [Curation] No STATdx image in this bundle replaces either of these specific shunt/collateral routes. Preserve both RadPrimer examples.

4. Hepatic Trauma
- [RadPrimer] Injuries commonly involve segments VI–VIII. Hematoma can track along the portal triad as a linear, focal or diffuse periportal lesion. Look for subcapsular hematoma, hemoperitoneum and accompanying renal or splenic injury; MDCT better evaluates trauma.
- [RadPrimer] The source describes hematoma echogenicity evolving from initially echogenic to hypoechoic after 4–5 days, with internal echoes/septa after 1–4 weeks. These are source-described temporal patterns, not time points documented by an image series in this bundle.
- [STATdx] Blood tracking along portal tracts may mimic dilated ducts or a deep laceration. Periportal blood usually accompanies another sign of hepatic injury and is generally limited to the lobe with hematoma/laceration. STATdx image 25 shows left-lobe laceration/hematoma and tracking blood after a motor vehicle crash.
- [STATdx] Rapid resuscitation can separately cause systemic hypervolemia and periportal lymphedema; STATdx images 3–5 illustrate this mechanism. Do not equate every post-trauma periportal collar with hemorrhage.

5. Acute Viral Hepatitis
- [RadPrimer] Hepatomegaly and decreased background echogenicity can accentuate echogenic portal-triad walls, producing the starry-sky pattern. Echogenic fat can be conspicuous in periportal tissues, ligamentum venosum and the falciform ligament. The source also describes periportal hypo-/anechoic areas with hydropic hepatocyte swelling.
- [STATdx] Acute hepatitis from viral, alcoholic or toxic causes may produce marked periportal and/or gallbladder-wall edema. STATdx images 8 and 9 are an explicit same-patient pair: periportal edema and porta-hepatis nodes, followed by the companion view of gallbladder-wall edema and a small amount of ascites. No interval or treatment response is supplied.

6. Fatty Sparing, Liver
- [RadPrimer] A focal hypoechoic region within an otherwise echogenic fatty liver, traversed by undisplaced vessels without mass effect, favors focal sparing. Typical sites are adjacent to the gallbladder and segment IV/anterior to the portal bifurcation. The source associates these sites with cystic and aberrant gastric venous drainage, respectively.
- [STATdx] Perivascular steatosis itself can mimic periportal fluid on CT and appear round when viewed in short axis near the dome. US/MR can distinguish fat from fluid. STATdx image 30 shows perivascular steatosis with relative sparing beside the gallbladder fossa.
- [Source qualification] RadPrimer's separate sentence attributing fatty sparing to “direct drainage of hepatic blood into systemic circulation” is retained in the original input only. Its mechanism is not clarified or corroborated by the paired text; do not turn that isolated wording into an unqualified mechanism claim. Keep the supported pattern, sites and absence of mass effect.

7. Diffuse/Infiltrative Hepatic Lymphoma
- [RadPrimer] Subcentimeter periportal hypoechoic foci may have a miliary distribution. Look for lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly/splenic lesions, bowel thickening and ascites. RadPrimer image 5 shows markedly hypoechoic, predominantly hypovascular periportal masses.
- [STATdx] Malignancy may increase lymphatic output, encase vessels or ducts, and cause biliary obstruction. Encasement can occur without vascular occlusion/thrombosis. STATdx image 33 shows periportal intermediate-signal infiltrative tissue in CLL without venous invasion.
- [Curation] Preserve the separate diagnoses and cases: ultrasound lymphoma and MR CLL are conceptual reinforcement, not the same patient or an identical image.

8. Pneumobilia
- [RadPrimer] Linear, intensely echogenic foci follow the biliary tree, often with posterior shadowing and reverberation. Gas rises to the nondependent liver, including the left lobe in a supine patient, and may change location with patient position. Causes include recent stone passage or instrumentation, a choledochoenteric fistula, and gas-forming biliary infection. RadPrimer image 6 provides the ultrasound recognition pattern.
- [STATdx] STATdx image 13 demonstrates ductal gas in the post-Whipple cholangitis/abscess case; STATdx image 19 demonstrates gas in markedly dilated ducts with recurrent cholangitis and abscess. Retain each clinical context. These CT appearances supplement the ultrasound artifact pattern.

9. Choledocholithiasis
- [RadPrimer] Echogenic stones along the biliary tree may shadow; small stones below 5 mm or soft pigmented stones may not. Large stones may obstruct with upstream dilation. RadPrimer image 7 directly depicts common-duct stones, upstream dilation and the avascular duct beside flowing vessels on Doppler.
- [STATdx] STATdx image 1 demonstrates the one-sided relationship of dilated ducts to portal branches in a patient with an obstructing ductal stone. STATdx images 17 and 18 show numerous intrahepatic calculi in recurrent pyogenic cholangitis; preserve the different stone location.
- [STATdx] The text offers duct diameter greater than half the neighboring portal-vein branch diameter as a clue to dilation. It is a source-specific morphologic clue, not a standalone diagnostic rule supplied with performance data.
- [Curation] The MR images add ductal anatomy and intrahepatic stone burden; they do not replace the direct common-duct-stone Doppler example.

10. Metastases
- [RadPrimer] Hepatic metastases are usually multiple and may occur anywhere in the liver. RadPrimer image 8 specifically shows ovarian-cancer metastasis infiltrating the left periportal region and occluding the left portal vein.
- [STATdx] Malignancy can produce edema, ductal obstruction or vascular/biliary encasement. STATdx image 21 concerns prior resection for metastases and subsequent chemotherapy-related duct injury; it is not an image of the active periportal metastasis shown by RadPrimer. STATdx images 31 and 33 concern HCC and CLL, respectively, and must not be relabeled as metastases.
- [Curation] Retain RadPrimer image 8 as an uncovered diagnosis-specific image.

Helpful Clues for Less Common Diagnoses — RadPrimer order

11. Peribiliary Cyst
- [RadPrimer] Small, well-defined cysts lie beside portal triads, usually multiply and especially in cirrhosis; they do not communicate with the biliary tree. RadPrimer image 9 shows cysts along the left portal vein on Doppler US.
- [STATdx] Cirrhosis can produce peribiliary cysts from dilated peribiliary glands. STATdx images 26 and 27 are an explicit same-patient CT/T2 MR pair showing water-density/water-signal cystic and tubular lesions paralleling portal veins and mimicking dilated ducts. Preserve all three modality examples.

12. Hepatic Schistosomiasis
- [RadPrimer] Periportal fibrosis is most marked at the porta hepatis, widening portal tracts. Echogenic thickened portal-venule walls produce clay-pipestem fibrosis; an anechoic vein surrounded by an echogenic fibrous mantle produces a bull's-eye pattern.
- [RadPrimer] Echogenic septa can outline polygonal relatively normal liver in a mosaic pattern. Complete septal fibrosis reflects inflammation/fibrosis around embolized eggs; incomplete septa or calcification may create mottled, nodular or sieve-like appearances. RadPrimer image 10 shows the echogenic mantle encasing portal veins; retain the source courtesy credit to W. Chong, MD.
- [Curation] No STATdx image in this bundle demonstrates schistosomiasis. PSC-related fibrosis is a different diagnosis and does not replace this example.

13. Recurrent Pyogenic Cholangitis
- [RadPrimer] The lateral left-lobe and posterior right-lobe segments are favored. Active infection can cause periportal hypo-/hyperechogenicity, thickened duct walls, and floating inflammatory echoes in dilated ducts. Late disease may cause lobar/segmental atrophy, biliary cirrhosis and crowded stone-filled ducts resembling a heterogeneous mass; stones may cast the duct.
- [RadPrimer] RadPrimer images 11 and 12 are an explicit same-patient grayscale/Doppler pair: stones, sludge and periductal inflammation, followed by absent flow within the duct. The color companion adds vascular-versus-biliary information and remains essential.
- [STATdx] The article describes an association with malnutrition and Asian origin; preserve this as source context rather than making demographic features diagnostic requirements. Ducts may be markedly distended with pus and calculi, with coexisting edema.
- [STATdx] STATdx images 17 and 18 provide complementary coronal and axial MR views of intrahepatic calculi and dilation; keep as a conservative companion group without asserting proven patient identity. STATdx image 19 adds the distinct CT appearance of marked dilation, ductal gas and abscess in a patient with recurrent sepsis.

14. Iatrogenic Material
- [RadPrimer] Shunts, stents, embolization material, drains and staples may appear as highly reflective echogenic material with a smooth surface or outline.
- [STATdx] STATdx image 21 includes hepatic surgical clips and a right-lobectomy history, but its principal finding is irregular duct dilation from hepatic intraarterial chemotherapy. STATdx image 22 provides another chemotherapy-related ductal appearance. Preserve these treatment contexts without relabeling them as a RadPrimer hardware image.

15. Caroli Disease
- [RadPrimer] Saccular or fusiform anechoic ductal dilations may partly or completely surround small portal-vein branches, producing the central dot sign.
- [Curation] This diagnosis is supported by RadPrimer text but has no explicitly labeled image in either staged set. Do not relabel peribiliary cyst or ordinary obstructive-dilation images as Caroli disease.

16. Hepatic Artery Calcification
- [RadPrimer] Branching linear echogenic structures track along portal triads; splenic and other small arteries may also be heavily calcified. Source-listed settings include longstanding diabetes, chronic renal failure and other causes of heavy vascular calcification. RadPrimer image 13 shows the pattern in end-stage renal disease.
- [Curation] No STATdx image supplies this specific vascular-calcification example.

17. Cystic Duct Remnant
- [RadPrimer] After cholecystectomy, a dilated remnant cystic duct can appear cystic near the porta hepatis. RadPrimer image 14 preserves the postoperative history and Doppler localization.
- [Curation] No STATdx image demonstrates a cystic-duct remnant. Peribiliary cysts and dilated intrahepatic ducts do not replace it.

=== STATDX SUPPLEMENTAL DEPTH — UNDER THE SAME CANONICAL TOPIC ===
These are supplemental mechanisms, mimics and diagnostic contexts. They do not replace RadPrimer's Common/Less Common ranking or create another root hierarchy.

Systemic Hypervolemia
- [STATdx] Overhydration, including rapid trauma resuscitation, can increase lymph production beyond drainage capacity. Look for a distended IVC and a collar of periportal edema. STATdx image 3 is a 22-year-old woman's case. STATdx images 4 and 5 describe an 18-year-old male after trauma/resuscitation; retain both distinct slices as a conservative same-case group.

Passive Hepatic Congestion
- [STATdx] Heart failure, constrictive pericarditis and tricuspid incompetence may produce periportal lymphedema. Distended hepatic veins/IVC and arterial-phase reflux of contrast into these veins are supportive source clues. STATdx images 6 and 7 preserve periportal and hepatic-venous views in a 78-year-old woman with heart failure; retain together without inventing an interval.

Additional Cholangitis Patterns
- [STATdx] Primary sclerosing cholangitis: irregular multifocal intra-/extrahepatic strictures with interspersed normal or dilated segments create beading. Dilation may mimic or cause edema; wall thickening/periportal edema may be striking on US. Ulcerative colitis is an association. STATdx images 14 and 15 are explicitly the same patient, comparing MRCP ductal beading with T1 periportal fibrosis. Preserve STATdx image 16 as a conservative associated ERCP procedure view, but patient identity across to that image is not explicitly established.
- [STATdx] AIDS-related cholangiopathy: opportunistic biliary infection may mimic PSC, with papillary stenosis, tapered distal CBD narrowing, proximal dilation, duct-wall thickening and periductal US changes. STATdx image 20 shows the MRCP pattern. The source's numerical MR/MRCP performance ranges are not promoted into general claims because their study/population basis is absent from the staged extraction.
- [STATdx] Chemotherapy-induced cholangitis: typically associated in the source with direct hepatic-artery catheter infusion, and may resemble PSC with stenosis/obstruction, mural thickening/enhancement, edema and adjacent hepatoduodenal-ligament fat stranding. Preserve STATdx images 21 and 22 as conservative treatment-context companions; a shared patient and acquisition interval are not explicitly documented.

Posttransplant Liver
- [STATdx] Severed lymphatics can cause periportal lymphedema while connections reform. It may persist for weeks and is not itself a sign of rejection. STATdx image 24 illustrates this source-described postoperative pattern; it does not establish the absence of every other graft complication.
- [STATdx] Bile ducts depend on arterial supply, so hepatic-artery stenosis/thrombosis can produce biliary necrosis before hepatic infarction. Branching or spherical collections may parallel ducts. STATdx image 23 shows indistinct dilated ducts, intrahepatic bilomas and ascites in that complication context. Preserve the distinction between expected edema and biliary injury; these are separate examples, not an asserted progression in one patient.

Porta Hepatis Lymphadenopathy, Cirrhosis and Portal Vein Thrombosis
- [STATdx] Porta-hepatis nodes can impair lymphatic drainage. Cirrhosis can produce true edema or mimics, including peribiliary cysts, portal obstruction and cavernous collaterals. Thrombosis may follow cirrhosis/portal hypertension, hypercoagulability or thrombophlebitis such as from diverticulitis.
- [STATdx] Nonenhancing branches in the portal-vein course can mimic ducts. Preserve the explicit same-patient STATdx images 28 and 29 with the post-bone-marrow-transplant hypercoagulable context, alongside the distinct Doppler cavernous-transformation example.

Hepatocellular Carcinoma and Cholangiocarcinoma
- [STATdx] HCC may produce nonspecific edema or invade/obstruct a bile duct or portal vein. Portal-vein expansion, enhancing tumor thrombus and continuity with a liver tumor favor tumor invasion. STATdx image 31 shows the enhancing main-portal-vein thrombus, thrombosed right portal vein and HCC; do not confuse it with bland thrombosis or ovarian metastasis.
- [STATdx] Hilar/peripheral cholangiocarcinoma can produce dilated ducts, edema or thickened duct walls. STATdx image 32 shows ductal obstruction near the right/left duct confluence. This supplements the broader malignant differential without changing RadPrimer's image labels.

Hepatic Inflammatory Pseudotumor
- [STATdx] A lymphoplasmacytic hilar pseudotumor can resemble a Klatskin tumor, with a short stricture and upstream dilation. The source recommends considering imaging, clinical and serum IgG4 evidence for a multisystem sclerosing disorder, including pancreatic, biliary or pulmonary involvement.
- [STATdx] STATdx image 34 was interpreted as cholangiocarcinoma because periportal tissue encased/narrowed the right portal vein; right hepatectomy instead demonstrated inflammatory pseudotumor. Preserve the pathology outcome and diagnostic uncertainty. The caption does not itself prove IgG4-related disease in this patient.
