=== DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS BACKBONE ===
- [RadPrimer] Biliary tract: gallstones, porcelain gallbladder, gallbladder carcinoma.
- [RadPrimer] Hepatic and splenic: calcified hepatic masses, mucinous metastases, splenic/hepatic granulomas, splenic cysts, echinococcal cysts, ischemia/infarct.
- [RadPrimer] Pancreatic: chronic pancreatitis and calcified pancreatic masses including solid pseudopapillary neoplasm, mucinous cystic neoplasm, pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor, serous cystadenoma, and pseudocyst.
- [RadPrimer] Urinary tract/adrenal: urolithiasis, calyceal diverticulum, calcified renal masses, renal cysts/polycystic kidney disease, renal TB, adrenal parenchymal calcification, calcified adrenal masses, prostatic/vas deferens calcification, bladder calculi, and bladder cancer.
- [RadPrimer] Female reproductive organs: calcified uterine leiomyoma, ovarian dermoid/teratoma, ovarian carcinoma.
- [RadPrimer] Abdominal wall/soft tissue: injection granulomas, costal cartilage mimic, calcified/ossified scar, calcinosis syndromes.
- [RadPrimer] Vascular: arterial calcification/aneurysm, Monckeberg calcification, phleboliths, chronic deep venous thrombosis.
- [RadPrimer] Bowel/peritoneum/mesentery: appendicolith, appendiceal mucocele, calcified lymph nodes, sclerosing peritonitis, pseudomyxoma peritonei, lymphangioma, sclerosing mesenteritis.

=== KEY FACTS ===
- [Both] Abdominal calcifications visible on radiographs or CT can be grouped by mechanism and morphology rather than by organ alone.
- [Both] Metastatic calcification is calcium salt deposition in otherwise normal tissue, classically with hypercalcemia and alkaline pH.
- [Both] Dystrophic calcification occurs in damaged tissue after trauma, ischemia, infarction, infection, or tumor.
- [Both] Ossification is true bone formation in damaged tissue, such as teratoma or ossified incision scar.
- [Both] Concretions form inside vessels or hollow viscera, including gallstones, renal calculi, and phleboliths; they often have a continuous rim or laminated edge.
- [Both] Conduit-wall calcification follows tubular structures, producing ringlike or tram-track morphology in arteries, aneurysms, veins, or vas deferens.
- [Both] Cystic-wall calcification appears as smooth curvilinear calcification around a fluid-filled mass and is larger than conduit-wall calcification.
- [Both] Solid-mass calcification is the most varied category; distribution and matrix help narrow the differential.

=== ORGAN-BASED CLUES ===
- [Both] Gallstones vary widely in density. Only a minority are visible on radiographs; calcium bilirubinate stones are more likely to be densely calcified, while cholesterol stones may be isoattenuating to bile.
- [Both] Gallstones may be laminated, faceted, or show internal gas. Dropped gallstones after laparoscopic cholecystectomy often settle in dependent recesses such as Morison pouch.
- [Both] Porcelain gallbladder is gallbladder wall calcification. The association with gallbladder carcinoma is now considered controversial, and CT is more sensitive than radiography.
- [Both] Calcified hepatic masses include hemangioma, adenoma after hemorrhage, fibrolamellar HCC with central scar calcification, conventional HCC, and especially mucinous metastases such as colon cancer.
- [Both] Splenic and hepatic granulomas appear as multiple punctate calcifications from healed granulomatous infection, commonly histoplasmosis or TB.
- [Both] Acquired splenic cysts are more likely than congenital cysts to show peripheral wall calcification.
- [Both] Chronic echinococcal cysts can show extensive whorled or serpiginous internal calcification; STATdx also shows rim calcification with daughter cysts.
- [Both] Chronic pancreatitis produces parenchymal and intraductal calcifications, often with pancreatic atrophy and ductal change.
- [Both] Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor is the solid pancreatic mass most likely to calcify, usually centrally and coarsely.
- [Both] Mucinous cystic neoplasm, solid pseudopapillary neoplasm, serous cystadenoma, and pseudocyst may calcify; MCN often has peripheral curvilinear calcification and serous cystadenoma can calcify in a central scar.
- [Both] Renal calculi are opaque on CT except indinavir stones; radiographs miss lucent urate stones and smaller stones.
- [Both] Calcified renal masses include RCC, Wilms tumor, and multilocular cystic nephroma; RCC calcification is reported in about 25% of cases.
- [Both] Renal cyst calcification is often thin or smooth, but thick, irregular, or nodular calcification raises Bosniak concern.
- [Both] Chronic renal TB can cause an atrophic, calcified nonfunctioning kidney, sometimes called putty kidney.
- [Both] Adrenal calcification usually reflects old hemorrhage or infection, especially TB or histoplasmosis; adrenal masses such as myelolipoma, adrenocortical carcinoma, pheochromocytoma, and adrenal cyst can calcify.
- [Both] Vas deferens calcification is paired tubular pelvic conduit calcification, most often in insulin-dependent diabetes or older patients.
- [Both] Bladder stones usually reflect urinary stasis or migration from the upper tract; they may be large, laminated, rounded, or spiculated.
- [Both] Uterine fibroid calcification may be whorled, flocculent, heterogeneous, or popcorn-like.
- [Both] Dermoids often contain tooth or bone calcification in a mural nodule, frequently with fat or a fat-fluid level.
- [Both] Epithelial ovarian neoplasms, especially serous malignancies, can calcify and may have calcified peritoneal implants.
- [Both] Injection granulomas are typically subcutaneous/buttock calcifications and may mimic intraabdominal calcifications on radiographs.
- [Both] Calcinosis syndromes can produce extensive soft-tissue and muscular calcification in connective tissue disease, including scleroderma, CREST, lupus, and dermatomyositis.
- [Both] Phleboliths are pelvic venous calcifications, often below the iliac spines and sometimes centrally lucent.
- [Both] Chronic deep venous thrombosis can calcify the IVC and iliac veins as linear venous calcification.
- [Both] Appendicolith is hardened calcified fecal material and may accompany appendicitis; appendiceal mucocele may show curvilinear wall calcification.
- [Both] Sclerosing peritonitis from prior peritoneal dialysis manifests as peritoneal thickening and calcification, sometimes encasing bowel.
- [Both] Pseudomyxoma peritonei can have punctate or curvilinear mucinous implant calcifications; lymphangioma can show fine wall or septal calcifications.
- [Both] End-stage sclerosing mesenteritis can form a left upper quadrant calcified mesenteric mass with tethered bowel and vessels.
