MASTER SUMMARY
- [Both] A hemorrhagic ovarian cyst is a physiologic ovarian cyst, most often a corpus luteum cyst, with internal hemorrhage. Hemorrhage may also occur into a follicular cyst, and acute hemorrhage can occasionally occur into an established endometrioma.
- [Both] The central recognition pattern is an intraovarian or exophytic ovarian cyst with avascular internal blood products: fine lace-like or reticular fibrin strands, fishnet/cobweb echoes, retracting clot, clot fragments with angular or concave margins, or a fluid-fluid/debris level.
- [Both] Color Doppler should show no vascularity within clot or fibrin strands. Peripheral wall or corpus luteum flow can be prominent and can create a ring-of-fire appearance.
- [Both] Most hemorrhagic ovarian cysts resolve or substantially decrease within about 6-8 weeks, and most leave no sequela. Larger cysts are more likely to cause acute pain, rupture, hemoperitoneum, or serve as a lead point for torsion.

ULTRASOUND RECOGNITION
- [Both] Endovaginal ultrasound with color Doppler is the best imaging test. Confirm ovarian origin by identifying surrounding ovarian tissue, a claw sign, or motion with the ovary rather than separation from the ovary under gentle probe pressure.
- [RadPrimer] RadPrimer image 1 shows the canonical grayscale lace-like internal echoes and posterior acoustic enhancement of a complex right ovarian hemorrhagic cyst.
- [RadPrimer] RadPrimer image 2 adds the Doppler rule: the cyst contents are avascular while displaced peripheral ovarian parenchyma shows flow and a venous waveform.
- [STATdx] STATdx emphasizes four common grayscale patterns: diffuse echogenicity that can resemble dermoid, reticular fibrin strands, retracting avascular clot with convex/concave/straight margins, and a simple-appearing cyst with a fluid-fluid level.
- [Both] Posterior acoustic enhancement or through transmission helps confirm a cystic lesion when blood products make it look solid. Internal Doppler vascularity or papillary projections argue against uncomplicated hemorrhagic cyst.
- [Both] Rupture may cause echogenic or variably echogenic pelvic free fluid. If fluid is substantial, scan the upper abdomen, including the right kidney/Morrison pouch region and subphrenic spaces, to assess hemoperitoneum extent.
- [RadPrimer] RadPrimer image 3 shows complex cul-de-sac fluid from rupture, and RadPrimer image 7 shows extension of fluid to the hepatorenal fossa.

CT AND MR
- [Both] CT may show an adnexal cyst with high-attenuation blood products, a fluid-fluid level, peripheral enhancement, and hemoperitoneum. CT is often obtained first in acute pelvic pain, but ultrasound better defines internal cyst architecture.
- [STATdx] The CT sentinel clot sign can help localize the ovarian bleeding source: fluid around the ovary is higher attenuation than fluid farther away. STATdx images 21 and 22 show ruptured cyst CT examples, including a wall defect in one case.
- [RadPrimer] RadPrimer images 8 and 9 show large-volume CT hemoperitoneum; RadPrimer image 9 also shows active bleeding in the right adnexal region on coronal CT.
- [Both] MR blood-product signal is variable. Classic teaching is T1 and T2 hyperintensity, but STATdx stresses that signal depends on blood age, hematocrit, protein concentration, hemoglobin form, and clot formation or retraction.
- [Both] T1 fat-suppressed imaging is useful because persistent high signal after fat suppression supports blood products rather than fat-containing dermoid. RadPrimer image 10 is the canonical selected T1 fat-suppressed example; STATdx image 18 is kept as the T2 companion image.

DIFFERENTIAL AND PITFALLS
- [Both] Endometrioma is a major mimic. It more often has uniform low-level or ground-glass echoes, wall echogenic foci, chronic cyclic pain/infertility, and little change on follow-up; a hemorrhagic cyst should resolve or shrink. Acute hemorrhage into an endometrioma can overlap, with follow-up showing clot resolution but persistent background endometrioma.
- [Both] Solid ovarian mass or ovarian cancer is suggested by papillary projections, mural nodules, thick septa, absence of cystic through-transmission, and internal vascularity. Angular avascular clot fragments can simulate solid tissue but should not enhance or vascularize internally.
- [Both] Ectopic pregnancy remains a critical mimic when ring-of-fire flow is seen. A corpus luteum or hemorrhagic corpus luteum is intraovarian and moves with the ovary; a tubal ectopic is typically extraovarian and may separate from the ovary with probe pressure.
- [STATdx] Dermoid cyst can mimic a reticular pattern through hair mesh, but other signs such as Rokitansky nodule, calcification, or fat-fluid level should be present.
- [Both] Torsion may coexist with or complicate a hemorrhagic cyst. Look for enlarged tender ovary, peripheral follicles, parenchymal inhomogeneity, abnormal position, twisted pedicle, and abnormal venous/arterial flow; normal flow does not fully exclude torsion.
- [Both] Pelvic abscess or tuboovarian abscess is favored by fever, leukocytosis, marked tenderness, inflammatory loss of tissue planes, pyosalpinx, purulent discharge, and possible perihepatic inflammatory change.

PATHOLOGY AND CLINICAL COURSE
- [Both] Most hemorrhagic ovarian cysts result from bleeding into a functional follicular or corpus luteum cyst, commonly at ovulation. Grossly, the cyst may be thin or thick walled and contain clot or serosanguineous fluid.
- [Both] Presentation ranges from incidental finding to acute pelvic pain. Severe pain often improves within hours, and most masses disappear within about 8 weeks.
- [RadPrimer] Hemorrhagic cysts may complicate ovulation induction or assisted reproduction and can be confused clinically with torsion or ovarian hyperstimulation. Fetal ovarian cysts may hemorrhage or torse, with an in-utero fluid-fluid level being suspicious.
- [STATdx] Late postmenopausal patients should not ovulate; a hemorrhagic-appearing cystic lesion in that setting should prompt concern for malignancy rather than routine physiologic-cyst follow-up.

MANAGEMENT ORIENTATION
- [Both] SRU-style management for classic hemorrhagic cysts in reproductive-age patients: 5 cm or smaller needs no follow-up; larger than 5 cm gets short-interval ultrasound follow-up in about 6-12 weeks, ideally during the follicular phase when feasible.
- [Both] Early postmenopausal hemorrhagic-appearing cysts should get follow-up ultrasound to document resolution. Late postmenopausal hemorrhagic-appearing cysts should be considered for surgical evaluation.
- [Both] Rupture with hemoperitoneum usually receives supportive management if the patient is hemodynamically stable. Surgery is reserved for hemodynamic instability, significant intraperitoneal hemorrhage, torsion, or severe persistent symptoms.
- [RadPrimer] Recurrent hemorrhagic cysts may be managed with ovulation suppression.
