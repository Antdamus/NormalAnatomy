MASTER SUMMARY
- [Both] Testicular carcinoma is usually a malignant germ cell tumor presenting as a discrete intratesticular mass; ultrasound is the primary diagnostic test, while CT abdomen/pelvis, chest imaging, selected MR, and selected PET/bone/brain imaging support staging and restaging.
- [RadPrimer] The key imaging clue is a hypoechoic or mixed echogenic testicular mass, often with abnormal vascularity. A discrete grayscale mass with intrinsic vessels should be treated as suspicious for malignancy.
- [STATdx] AJCC staging applies to postpubertal germ cell tumors and malignant sex cord-stromal tumors; it does not apply to spermatocytic tumor, nonmalignant sex cord-stromal tumor, prepubertal germ cell tumor, hematolymphoid tumor, or paratesticular neoplasm.
- [Both] Germ cell tumors account for most malignant testicular tumors. Seminoma is commonly homogeneous, hypoechoic, solid, and vascular; nonseminomatous tumors are more likely heterogeneous, ill-defined, cystic, calcified, hemorrhagic, necrotic, or invasive.

PRIMARY TUMOR DETECTION AND LOCAL CHARACTERIZATION
- [RadPrimer] Ultrasound identifies and characterizes the scrotal mass. Use a high-frequency linear transducer. Most tumors are unilateral; contralateral tumor can occur, usually asynchronously.
- [STATdx] Ultrasound is nearly 100% sensitive for detecting scrotal masses and usually distinguishes intratesticular from extratesticular masses, but it cannot reliably subtype most tumors; orchiectomy provides definitive staging and histology.
- [Both] Seminoma tends to be well-defined, hypoechoic, solid, and relatively homogeneous; larger seminomas may be heterogeneous or lobulated. Doppler commonly shows increased vascularity compared with normal testis.
- [Both] Teratoma and mixed nonseminomatous tumors often show complex cystic/solid architecture, calcification, cartilage/bone elements, fibrosis, hemorrhage, or necrosis.
- [Both] Embryonal carcinoma is often heterogeneous, poorly marginated, predominantly solid, and may invade the tunica albuginea or distort the testicular contour.
- [Both] Choriocarcinoma can be small and echogenic but is notable for hemorrhage/necrosis/calcification and early hematogenous metastasis, especially to lung and brain.
- [STATdx] MR is not routine for initial evaluation, but can help in complicated cases and can demonstrate tunica albuginea/vaginalis, spermatic cord, and scrotal invasion. Seminoma on MR is often sharply defined, multinodular, T2 hypointense, and may show enhancing fibrovascular septa; NSGCT is more heterogeneous and ill-defined.

STAGING FRAMEWORK
- [STATdx] Imaging is not the standard method for pathologic T staging; radical inguinal orchiectomy with pathologic assessment determines local tumor extent and histologic subtype composition.
- [STATdx] T1 disease is limited to testis including rete testis invasion, without lymphovascular invasion; pure seminoma is subdivided by size into pT1a under 3 cm and pT1b at least 3 cm.
- [STATdx] T2 disease is limited to testis with lymphovascular invasion, hilar soft tissue/epididymal invasion, or penetration of the visceral mesothelial layer covering the external tunica albuginea.
- [STATdx] T3 disease invades the spermatic cord. T4 disease invades the scrotum.
- [STATdx] CT abdomen/pelvis is indicated for initial regional nodal staging. Suspicious nodes are low attenuation or poorly enhancing in typical drainage locations; an 8-mm short-axis threshold in typical retroperitoneal locations is specific but insensitive.
- [STATdx] N1 means single or multiple regional nodes up to 2 cm short axis; N2 means nodes over 2 cm and up to 5 cm; N3 means nodes over 5 cm.
- [STATdx] Nonregional nodal or pulmonary metastases define M1a disease. Nonpulmonary visceral metastases, including liver, brain, bone, kidney, or adrenal disease, define M1b disease.
- [STATdx] Serum marker category is part of post-orchiectomy staging: LDH, beta-hCG, and AFP define S0-S3 severity groups. Elevated markers after orchiectomy can make stage IS disease even when imaging is negative.

NODAL AND METASTATIC SPREAD
- [Both] Testicular germ cell tumors usually spread first by lymphatics to retroperitoneal nodes. Right testicular tumors first involve right-sided nodes around the IVC, commonly aortocaval/paracaval nodes. Left testicular tumors first involve paraaortic/preaortic nodes in the region bounded by the renal vein, aorta, ureter, and inferior mesenteric artery.
- [STATdx] Inguinal or pelvic nodes are regional only when lymphatics have been disrupted by prior scrotal/inguinal surgery or tumor-contaminated scrotum; otherwise isolated inguinal nodes should be considered nonregional disease.
- [STATdx] Above-diaphragm nodal disease can spread via thoracic duct in seminoma; NSGCT spread is more random and can involve anterior mediastinal, aortopulmonary window, hilar, supraclavicular, and neck nodes.
- [Both] Hematogenous spread is late except for choriocarcinoma. Lung is the most common distant site; liver, brain, bone, kidney, and adrenal metastases are also important.
- [RadPrimer] CT/MR evaluate nodal and metastatic disease; PET can help evaluate post-treatment residual masses, especially seminoma, but negative PET does not exclude teratoma/NSGCT residual disease.

STAGE GROUPS AND RISK
- [RadPrimer] Stage I is confined to testis; stage II involves nodes below the diaphragm; stage III involves lymph nodes above the diaphragm or extranodal metastases.
- [STATdx] Stage IIA corresponds to limited N1 nodal disease; stage IIB corresponds to N2 disease; stage IIC corresponds to N3 disease. M1a disease includes nonregional nodes or pulmonary metastases. M1b disease includes nonpulmonary visceral metastases and is stage IIIC.
- [STATdx] Good-risk disease has no nonpulmonary visceral metastases and favorable marker/site profiles; intermediate and poor risk are driven by nonseminoma mediastinal primary, nonpulmonary visceral metastases, and higher AFP/beta-hCG/LDH categories.

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS AND PITFALLS
- [RadPrimer] Important mimics include epidermoid cyst, lymphoma, subacute hematoma, segmental infarct, and focal orchitis.
- [RadPrimer] Consider lymphoma in older patients, bilateral or multifocal lesions, and associated lymphadenopathy elsewhere.
- [STATdx] Burned-out germ cell tumor can present as a metastatic retroperitoneal mass with only a tiny scar, cystic focus, or calcified testicular lesion; always image the testes in the appropriate patient with retroperitoneal germ-cell-type disease.
- [STATdx] Microlithiasis without mass is controversial as a risk factor; most recommendations favor self-exam rather than routine ultrasound screening when no mass is present.

TREATMENT AND FOLLOW-UP ORIENTATION
- [Both] Radical inguinal orchiectomy is the diagnostic and initial therapeutic step for most suspected testicular carcinoma.
- [RadPrimer] Seminoma is radiosensitive and chemotherapy-sensitive. Nonseminomatous tumors are treated with orchiectomy, chemotherapy, and/or retroperitoneal lymph node dissection depending on stage and response.
- [STATdx] Stage and histology drive surveillance, radiation, chemotherapy, and residual-mass management. Seminoma residual masses with normal markers may be evaluated with FDG PET; NSGCT residual masses often require resection because teratoma can be PET-negative.
