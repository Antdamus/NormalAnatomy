## Fused Teaching Backbone

### Key Facts
- [Both] Nabothian cysts are mucus-filled dilatations of obstructed endocervical glands. STATdx emphasizes obstruction by squamous epithelial overgrowth at the gland neck; RadPrimer frames the same process as mucus retention within endocervical glands.
- [Both] Tunnel clusters are a subtype of nabothian cyst with complex multicystic dilatation of endocervical glands; they are classically associated with pregnancy/multigravidity and may penetrate deeper into the cervical stroma.
- [Both] Most nabothian cysts are benign, incidental, and asymptomatic. They are among the most common benign pathologic processes of the cervix.
- [Both] Typical imaging shows circumscribed, usually superficial, round/oval or unilocular cysts in the cervical wall along the endocervical canal or ectocervix. They are often multiple and usually small, but can enlarge the cervix or mimic a mass.
- [RadPrimer] Endovaginal/transvaginal ultrasound is the first-line imaging study. The key ultrasound task is to prove the lesion is within the cervix, define its relationship to the endocervical canal, and identify the separate ovary when a lateral cyst mimics an ovarian follicle.
- [STATdx] CT and MRI are supplemental characterization tools: CT shows nonenhancing low-attenuation cervical lesions, and MRI shows T2-hyperintense cysts with variable T1 mucinous signal and no postcontrast enhancement.
- [Both] Concerning features include solid components, irregular enhancement or flow, deep stromal invasion, large/multiloculated mass-like morphology, or copious watery vaginal discharge.

### Terminology and Pathophysiology
- [Both] Synonyms include endocervical gland cyst, retention cyst/mucus retention cyst, epithelial inclusion cyst, cervical cyst, ovula nabothi, and nabothian follicle.
- [Both] Nabothian cysts form when endocervical glands are covered or obstructed by squamous epithelium during healing/chronic cervicitis; columnar epithelium continues to secrete mucus, causing gland dilation.
- [RadPrimer] They may be associated with chronic cervicitis, postpartum/cervical healing, and progestogenic therapy with failure of normal cyclic mucus flow.
- [STATdx] Grossly they are yellow or white cysts on the cervical surface, frequently multiple, and filled with clear fluid. Microscopically, they are lined by low columnar mucin-producing epithelium.
- [Both] Tunnel clusters include type A small nondilated tunnels and type B cystically dilated tunnels; type B produces the gross cystic abnormality.

## Imaging Features

### Ultrasound
- [Both] Transvaginal/endovaginal ultrasound usually shows a well-defined anechoic cervical cyst with thin wall and posterior acoustic enhancement.
- [Both] Cysts may be single or multiple; multiple cysts can obscure the endocervical canal and mimic a cervical/adnexal cystic mass.
- [RadPrimer] Debris or internal echoes are common and benign, especially with proteinaceous or hemorrhagic content.
- [RadPrimer] Large cysts can displace the endocervical canal, rarely contribute to cervical stenosis, and mimic cervical os dilatation in pregnancy.
- [Both] Nabothian cysts should be avascular. Use color or power Doppler if a cyst appears solid or complex.
- [STATdx] Power Doppler is useful because adenoma malignum may show flow within the wall or cystic portion, whereas nabothian cysts show no flow.

### MRI
- [Both] MRI is helpful when ultrasound is technically limited, the lesion is large/complex/deep, or malignant mimic is a concern.
- [Both] T2-weighted MRI shows circumscribed hyperintense cervical cysts; deep tunnel clusters may appear as complex multicystic lesions penetrating the cervical stroma.
- [Both] T1 signal is variable and may be intermediate to hyperintense because of mucinous, proteinaceous, or hemorrhagic contents.
- [Both] Postcontrast T1-weighted MRI shows no internal enhancement; enhancement of solid elements should prompt concern for neoplasm.
- [RadPrimer] DWI may show diffusion restriction, so diffusion signal alone should not override the morphology and enhancement pattern.

### CT
- [Both] CT is not the preferred characterization study but may show a single or multicystic nonenhancing hypodense cervical lesion.
- [STATdx] On noncontrast CT, nabothian cysts may be isodense or hypodense to the cervix and can be hyperdense if complicated.
- [STATdx] Large or complex cysts on CT may mimic endocervical gland tumor and should be triaged to ultrasound/MRI correlation when needed.

## Differential Diagnosis
- [Both] Adenoma malignum/minimal deviation adenocarcinoma: low-grade mucinous carcinoma of deep endocervical glands; multilocular cystic cervical mass with deep stromal penetration, enhancing solid components or wall flow, and classic copious watery discharge. RadPrimer notes association with Peutz-Jeghers syndrome.
- [Both] Endocervical/cervical glandular hyperplasia: superficial inner-cervical mucosal thickening with possible cystic change; associated with pregnancy/peripartum state or progestational agents and can overlap with adenoma malignum.
- [RadPrimer] Uterine cervicitis: tenacious yellow/turbid jelly-like discharge and pelvic pressure; can appear as cervical retention cysts.
- [RadPrimer] Cystic endometrial polyp: cyst-containing endocavitary mass that may arise from or prolapse into the cervix, but is not centered in cervical stroma and may have a vascular stalk.
- [Both] Cervical cancer/squamous cell carcinoma: predominantly solid cervical mass, sometimes necrotic, with irregular enhancement or solid elements. RadPrimer notes bulky cervix, cervical stenosis with endometrial fluid, and mucin-producing carcinoma as a mimic.
- [RadPrimer] Cervical ectopic pregnancy: thick-walled cystic cervical stromal mass with vascular echogenic ring, high-velocity low-resistance trophoblastic flow, and possible yolk sac/embryo/cardiac activity; avoid dilation and curettage.
- [RadPrimer] Abortion in progress: teardrop-shaped gestational sac in the cervical canal, often without trophoblastic flow when nonviable; living embryo requires exclusion of cervical ectopic pregnancy.

## Clinical Issues and Management
- [Both] Most are incidental and asymptomatic; superficial cysts may be seen on speculum/colposcopic exam, while deeper cysts are found on imaging.
- [Both] Prevalence increases with age; STATdx reports about 8% of adult women and 13% of postmenopausal women.
- [Both] They are more common in women who have had children. Tunnel clusters are classically seen in multigravid women older than 30 years and often during pregnancy.
- [Both] Symptoms are uncommon but can occur from mass effect, infection, enlarged cervix, cervical stenosis, palpable large cyst, or chronic cervicitis.
- [Both] The great majority need no treatment. Symptomatic cases may be treated with drainage, cryotherapy/cryosurgery, electrocautery, or conization depending on clinical context.
