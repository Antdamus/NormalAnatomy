## Fused Teaching Backbone

### Definition And Purpose

- [Both] Hydronephrosis is dilation of the renal collecting system, often with renal enlargement, due to impaired urinary outflow; the cause may be mechanical/anatomic or functional.
- [Both] Mechanical causes include ectopic ureter, ureteral stenosis, UPJ stenosis, nephrolithiasis, urothelial or pelvic mass, prostate/bladder outlet pathology, ureterocele, posterior urethral valve, retroperitoneal fibrosis, and extrinsic adenopathy or mass.
- [Both] Functional or nonobstructive dilation can reflect abnormal peristalsis, patulous collecting systems after prior obstruction, urinary diversion, or persistent hydronephrosis after an obstruction has resolved.
- [Both] Tc-99m MAG3 renal scintigraphy characterizes hydronephrosis as obstructed versus dilated but nonobstructed and estimates differential renal function.
- [STATdx] Curves must be correlated with the raw images because duplicated collecting systems, ROI placement over bladder/diaper activity, motion, and anatomy can make curve-only interpretation misleading.

### Protocol And Measurement

- [Both] Patient preparation includes hydration, voiding before the study, and avoidance of furosemide/diuretics on the day of the exam; full bladder, incomplete voiding, bladder outlet obstruction, or reflux can complicate interpretation.
- [STATdx] Catheterization should be considered when voiding is unreliable, such as non-potty-trained children or neurogenic bladder.
- [Both] MAG3 is preferred for hydronephrosis evaluation because tubular extraction preserves image quality even with reduced GFR; DTPA is less robust in low GFR and is usually reserved for GFR calculations.
- [Both] Typical acquisition includes posterior supine flow images, dynamic imaging for 20-30 minutes, furosemide after the collecting system fills, and additional postfurosemide imaging for 20-30 minutes.
- [Both] Adults commonly receive 40 mg IV furosemide with normal creatinine; azotemia may require higher doses, and pediatric dosing is weight based.
- [Both] Differential renal function is normally about 45-55% per kidney. Reported values should be trended over time because loss of relative function can be more important than a single borderline value.
- [STATdx] Each exam offers three opportunities to clear a kidney or moiety: prefurosemide washout, postfurosemide washout, and gravity-assisted drainage/postvoid imaging. Once a kidney or moiety clears, later t1/2 values add little.

### Renogram Pattern Categories

- [Both] Normal: prompt uptake, cortical peak around 2-3 minutes, calyceal activity by about 5 minutes, bladder activity by 10-15 minutes, spontaneous washout before furosemide, and t1/2 <= 10 minutes.
- [Both] Dilated but unobstructed: collecting-system activity may rise until furosemide or GAD, then washes out normally; differential renal function is usually normal unless prior obstruction caused damage.
- [STATdx] GAD supports nonobstruction when clearance is > 50% and supports obstruction when clearance is < 50%; adding GAD improves specificity for furosemide renography.
- [Both] Partial obstruction: excretion is delayed and may improve but not normalize after furosemide or GAD; t1/2 is indeterminate at 10-20 minutes and obstructed when > 20 minutes, with higher t1/2 suggesting higher-grade obstruction.
- [Both] High-grade or complete obstruction: delayed/asymmetric flow, delayed or absent collecting-system visualization, low differential function, progressive collecting-system activity despite furosemide/GAD, and very high or unmeasurable t1/2.
- [Both] Severely dilated but nonobstructed systems may need a more permissive t1/2 cutoff around 15 minutes depending on the clinical scenario.

### Mimics, Pitfalls, And Differential

- [Both] Vesicoureteral reflux can mimic obstruction, especially if the bladder is not catheterized; reflux and obstruction can coexist.
- [Both] Acute tubular necrosis may show delayed nephrogram and minimal excretion, especially in transplant kidneys, but is not a collecting-system obstruction.
- [Both] Renal artery stenosis can cause asymmetric reduced flow, delayed nephrogram, smaller kidney, and low relative function while washout may remain normal.
- [STATdx] Renal vein thrombosis can mimic high-grade obstruction with reduced flow, delayed nephrogram, and poor collecting-system visualization.
- [STATdx] Low GFR, dehydration, poor response to furosemide, full bladder, immature kidneys, and atonic/severely dilated systems can create false-positive or indeterminate scans; many improve with GAD.
- [Both] CT, MR urography, ultrasound, and bone scan/PET examples provide anatomic or incidental correlation; anatomic dilation can persist after relief of obstruction, so functional assessment may still be needed.

### Clinical Course And Management

- [Both] High-grade obstruction requires treatment to avoid renal functional impairment. Mechanical relief options include stent, nephrostomy, pyeloplasty, urinary diversion, and lithotripsy depending on cause.
- [Both] Low-grade or partial obstruction is often followed with serial renograms to evaluate resolution and monitor for decreasing differential renal function.
- [STATdx] If GAD clearance is > 50% and function is stable, conservative management is supported; if clearance remains < 50% or differential renal function declines, obstruction is more likely clinically significant.
