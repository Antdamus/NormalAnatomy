=== DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS ===
- [RadPrimer] Common: Hepatic cyst; autosomal dominant polycystic disease, liver; hepatic pyogenic abscess; biliary hamartomas; hepatic metastases; hepatic amebic abscess; biloma/seroma; steatosis/fatty liver mimic.
- [RadPrimer] Less common: Hepatic candidiasis; hepatic hydatid cyst; biliary cystadenoma/carcinoma; biliary intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm; hepatocellular carcinoma; Caroli disease; undifferentiated hepatic sarcoma; intrahepatic pseudocyst; hepatic inflammatory pseudotumor; ciliated hepatic foregut cyst.

=== HIGH-YIELD DISCRIMINATORS ===
- [Both] Mural nodularity, enhancing wall/septa, internal debris, high-attenuation blood, bile-duct communication, and interval change are the main discriminators for cystic hepatic masses.
- [Both] Compare with prior studies: simple cysts change size slowly, abscesses can change quickly, and treated tumors such as GIST metastases may become cystic and simulate benign cysts.
- [Both] Multiplanar review matters: coronal and sagittal imaging can reveal morphology, true lesion number, biliary communication, daughter cysts, or surgical/traumatic context.
- [Both] A lesion that looks cystic but has a mural nodule, solid component, arterial hyperenhancement/washout, enhancing septa, or ductal surface nodularity should be treated as tumor until proven otherwise.
- [Both] Clinical setting is decisive for infection and collections: fever/immunosuppression suggests abscess/candidiasis; trauma, surgery, ablation, or transplant suggests biloma/seroma; endemic exposure plus serology supports hydatid or amebic disease.
- [Both] Steatosis is an important mimic: focal fat can approach water attenuation on noncontrast CT, but US echogenicity and opposed-phase MR signal dropout distinguish it from a true cystic mass.

=== DIAGNOSIS-SPECIFIC SYNTHESIS ===
- [Both] Hepatic cyst: Water attenuation, no visible wall, no enhancement, no mural nodularity, very bright T2/dark T1 signal, and sonolucent US with acoustic enhancement. One or two thin septa may occur. Hemorrhage can make a benign cyst difficult to separate from cystic neoplasm. Use STATdx images 1-3 for uncomplicated, obstructing, and hemorrhagic cyst variants.
- [Both] Autosomal dominant polycystic disease of the liver: Numerous cysts of varying size can produce massive hepatomegaly but rarely impair liver function. Diagnosis is not made from liver cysts alone; look for cysts in other organs, family history, or genetic testing. Use STATdx image 4 for the core pattern.
- [Both] Hepatic pyogenic abscess: Multiloculated/multiseptate complex cyst cluster with enhancing wall or septa; peripheral lesions near the diaphragm may have adjacent atelectasis or pleural effusion. Etiologies include surgery/trauma, cholecystitis/cholangitis, or hematogenous spread from colon. Use STATdx image 5 for complex abscess with aspiration/drainage context.
- [Both] Biliary hamartomas: Innumerable small low-attenuation lesions that mimic cysts on CT/MR, often 1-1.5 cm, sometimes with echogenic or enhancing wall nodules. Lack of larger cystic lesions and lack of extrahepatic cysts helps distinguish them from polycystic liver disease. Use STATdx images 6-8 as a CT/US/MRCP multimodality cluster.
- [Both] Hepatic metastases: Cystic metastases may arise from cystic primaries such as ovarian tumor, squamous cell metastases, mucinous adenocarcinoma, sarcomas, or treated GIST. Mural nodularity on CT, US, or MR is a key clue. Use STATdx image 9 for a cystic thyroid metastasis with mural nodule and STATdx images 10-11 as an atomic gastric GIST treatment-response cluster.
- [Both] Hepatic amebic abscess: Typically solitary, peripheral, round or ovoid, and diagnosis usually rests on imaging plus clinical presentation and serology. Use STATdx image 12 for the classic shaggy encapsulated cystic mass.
- [Both] Biloma/seroma: Occurs after trauma, partial liver resection, or radiofrequency ablation; uninfected collections may not require drainage and may take weeks to months to resolve. In a liver allograft, biloma from hepatic artery thrombosis with biliary necrosis is ominous. Use STATdx image 13 for posttraumatic biloma/seroma and STATdx images 14-15 as the transplant hepatic-artery-thrombosis cluster.
- [Both] Hepatic candidiasis: Innumerable microabscesses, often less than 1 cm, with target or wheel appearance in immunocompromised patients; fungal and mycobacterial opportunistic infection may overlap. Use STATdx image 16.
- [Both] Hepatic hydatid cyst: Solitary or multiple cysts with discrete peripheral wall with or without calcification; mother cyst contains hydatid matrix/sand and daughter cysts or scolices. Use STATdx image 17.
- [Both] Biliary cystadenoma/carcinoma: Usually a solitary, large, multiseptate mass with enhancing wall and septa, classically in middle-aged women. Complete resection is essential to prevent recurrence. Use STATdx image 18.
- [Both] Biliary intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm: Bile-duct tumor can produce mucin that distends ducts and simulates a cystic mass; nodular enhancing components raise concern for cholangiocarcinoma. Use STATdx image 19.
- [Both] Hepatocellular carcinoma: Spontaneous or treatment necrosis can simulate a cystic mass; solid arterial hyperenhancing components and delayed washout point back to HCC. No selected source image uniquely illustrates HCC in this bundle, so retain as a text differential.
- [Both] Caroli disease: Cystic intrahepatic bile-duct dilation communicates with bile ducts; MRCP or ERCP is recommended, and central dot sign reflects dilated ducts around portal vein radicles. Use STATdx image 20.
- [Both] Undifferentiated hepatic sarcoma: Rare aggressive primary hepatic tumor, usually a very large solitary encapsulated mass with peripheral hypervascular solid component, complex cystic spaces, focal hemorrhage, and possible paradoxical solid US/cystic CT-MR appearance from myxoid stroma. Use STATdx image 21.
- [Both] Intrahepatic pseudocyst and hepatic inflammatory pseudotumor: Pseudocyst can dissect along portal triads and should prompt search for pancreatitis or adjacent pancreatic cyst; inflammatory pseudotumor more often mimics cholangiocarcinoma with delayed enhancement but rarely appears multiseptate/cystic. Retain as text differentials unless later images are supplied.
- [Both] Ciliated hepatic foregut cyst: Rare congenital cyst, typically small and in segment IV, that may appear complex or solid on US. Use STATdx image 22.
