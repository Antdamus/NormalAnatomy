=== DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS ===
- [RadPrimer] Common: Focal nodular hyperplasia; hepatic cavernous hemangioma.
- [RadPrimer] Less common: Hepatocellular carcinoma; hepatic adenoma; hepatic metastases; fibrolamellar hepatocellular carcinoma; intrahepatic/peripheral cholangiocarcinoma; epithelioid hemangioendothelioma.
- [RadPrimer] Rare but important: Nodular regenerative hyperplasia, especially large regenerative nodules in Budd-Chiari syndrome.

=== HIGH-YIELD DISCRIMINATORS ===
- [Both] Start with scar size, scar calcification, enhancement pattern of the mass/scar, multiplicity, and associated findings such as vascular invasion, biliary obstruction, capsular retraction, or Budd-Chiari morphology.
- [Both] FNH and cholangiocarcinoma are usually solitary, fibrolamellar carcinoma is almost always solitary, and hemangiomas, metastases, epithelioid hemangioendothelioma, HCC, and nodular regenerative hyperplasia may be multiple depending on clinical setting.
- [Both] Calcified scar strongly favors fibrolamellar HCC or giant hemangioma. Calcification is uncommon/rare in FNH, conventional HCC, adenoma, cholangiocarcinoma, and most metastases, but mucinous metastases such as colon/ovarian cancer can calcify.
- [Both] A true scar should be separated from necrosis, hemorrhage, fibrosis, fat, or treated tumor. Adenoma and metastases often mimic scar because of hemorrhage, necrosis, fat, or fibrosis rather than a true central scar.

=== DIAGNOSIS-SPECIFIC SYNTHESIS ===
- [Both] Focal nodular hyperplasia: Larger lesions often have a small central scar and thin radiating septa. The mass enhances homogeneously in the arterial phase and becomes isoattenuating/isointense to liver on later phases. The scar is bright on T2, may show delayed enhancement with CT or intravascular MR agents, and does not enhance/retain gadoxetate on delayed hepatobiliary imaging. Use STATdx images 1-3 as the primary FNH teaching cluster.
- [Both] Hepatic cavernous hemangioma: Large or giant hemangiomas may have a fibrotic nonenhancing central scar that may calcify, while nonscar tissue shows peripheral nodular discontinuous enhancement with progressive fill-in. Very bright T2 signal supports hemangioma. Use STATdx images 4-6 as the primary hemangioma cluster.
- [Both] Conventional hepatocellular carcinoma: Large tumors, especially in a noncirrhotic liver, can resemble fibrolamellar HCC because they are heterogeneous, hypervascular, and may have washout with central necrosis or scar. Scar calcification is rare in conventional HCC. Vascular invasion and metastatic disease support HCC. Use STATdx images 7-8 for mosaic enhancement, necrosis/scar, and portal vein tumor invasion.
- [Both] Hepatic adenoma: Low-density or low-signal regions usually represent fat, necrosis, or old hemorrhage rather than a true scar. Chemical-shift MRI can show signal drop from fat. Calcification is uncommon. Eovist-enhanced MRI is most useful for distinction from FNH. Use STATdx images 9-10 as adenoma-pattern reinforcement for hemorrhage/necrosis/fibrosis and nonenhancing central abnormality.
- [Both] Hepatic metastases: Target appearance and central low density usually reflect necrosis or fibrosis rather than a true scar. Fibrous stroma may be prominent after treatment or in some primaries. Calcification is uncommon overall but can be seen with mucinous metastases such as colon or ovarian cancer. Use STATdx images 11-12 together as the colon-cancer metastasis cluster.
- [Both] Fibrolamellar HCC: Classically a large heterogeneous mass in a young patient, often with a large central scar that is calcified in more than half of cases. Aggressive features such as local vascular/biliary invasion and metastases are common at presentation. Use STATdx images 13-15 as the fibrolamellar cluster, including nodal disease and calcified scar.
- [Both] Intrahepatic/peripheral cholangiocarcinoma: Focal necrosis or fibrosis can mimic scar. Extensive fibrous stroma produces progressive delayed enhancement. Capsular retraction, hepatic volume loss, biliary obstruction, and portal venous obstruction are key supporting clues. Use STATdx images 16-17 as the CT/MR cholangiocarcinoma cluster.
- [Both] Epithelioid hemangioendothelioma: Usually multiple peripheral nodules that may coalesce. Target or lollipop appearance and capsular retraction are important clues. Use STATdx images 18-19 for target/capsular retraction and T2 central necrosis or scar.
- [Both] Nodular regenerative hyperplasia / large regenerative nodules: Multiacinar large regenerative nodules can have central scars, are usually multiple and less than 4 cm, and are classically associated with Budd-Chiari syndrome. Hepatobiliary agent retention supports functional hepatocytes with deficient biliary ducts. Use STATdx images 20-24 as the Budd-Chiari/NRH imaging-pathology cluster, including trichrome confirmation.
