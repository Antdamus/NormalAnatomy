=== DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS BACKBONE ===
- [RadPrimer] Common: inguinal hernia; femoral hernia; ventral hernia including epigastric, hypogastric, and incisional hernias; spigelian hernia; lumbar hernia; umbilical hernia; subcutaneous abdominal wall mass mimics; enterocutaneous fistula mimic.
- [RadPrimer] Less common: obturator hernia; traumatic abdominal wall hernia; sciatic hernia; perineal hernia; spermatic cord lipoma or liposarcoma mimic.
- [STATdx] Supplemental mimic depth: abdominal wall abscess near a repair site and abdominal wall metastasis near a colostomy can mimic or complicate abdominal wall hernia evaluation.

=== KEY FACTS ===
- [Both] CT is the most accurate modality for diagnosing abdominal wall hernias and complications.
- [Both] US can help in nonurgent outpatient evaluation, especially for reducibility and transient hernias when upright positioning or Valsalva can elicit the defect.
- [Both] CT should be first-line when the presentation is acute or there is concern for bowel involvement, obstruction, ischemia, incarceration, or strangulation.
- [Both] Every hernia should be assessed for contents, neck, reducibility when available, bowel obstruction, bowel wall compromise, and ischemia.
- [Both] Interparietal or interstitial hernia means the sac lies between abdominal wall muscle/fascial planes without entering subcutaneous tissues.
- [Both] Richter hernia involves only part of the bowel circumference, typically the antimesenteric border, so obstruction may be absent despite strangulation risk.

=== COMMON DIAGNOSIS CLUES ===
- [Both] Inguinal hernia is the most common external hernia, about 80% of cases. It lies in the groin anterior to the horizontal plane of the pubic tubercle and does not compress the femoral vessels. Direct hernias arise anteromedial to the inferior epigastric vessels; indirect hernias arise superomedial to them, are more common, and have more complications.
- [Both] Femoral hernia extends into the femoral canal medial to the femoral vein and inferior to the inferior epigastric vessels. The sac is posterior and lateral to the pubic tubercle, often compresses the femoral vein, and carries high incarceration/strangulation risk.
- [Both] Ventral hernia is a broad category for anterior or lateral abdominal wall hernias. Epigastric hernias pass through the linea alba above the umbilicus; hypogastric hernias occur below it; incisional and parastomal hernias occur at surgical defects.
- [Both] Ventral hernias often enlarge over time, so even asymptomatic cases may become surgical problems as complication risk rises.
- [Both] Spigelian hernia passes through the aponeurosis of the internal oblique and transversus abdominis along the lateral rectus margin, often near the arcuate line; strangulation/incarceration risk is high.
- [Both] Lumbar hernia passes through lumbar muscle or thoracolumbar fascia, often between the 12th rib and iliac crest. Most are acquired after surgery, especially renal surgery, and large necks make incarceration less common.
- [Both] Umbilical hernia passes through the umbilical ring. Congenital cases often resolve in childhood; acquired cases are associated with obesity, multiparity, and ascites.
- [Both] Subcutaneous abdominal wall mass mimics include hematoma, abscess, lymphadenopathy, soft tissue neoplasm, cryptorchidism, hydrocele, and varicocele; imaging should show no true fascial defect.
- [Both] Enterocutaneous fistula can simulate a hernia when gas or enteric contrast tracks from bowel into the abdominal wall, but careful imaging shows a tract rather than a true hernia sac.

=== LESS COMMON DIAGNOSIS CLUES ===
- [Both] Obturator hernia passes through the obturator foramen/canal and is classically seen in older or multiparous women; it has high incarceration, strangulation, and mortality risk.
- [Both] Traumatic abdominal wall hernia follows focal trauma, commonly lower abdominal or iliac crest injury, including pediatric handlebar injury and adult high-energy trauma.
- [Both] Sciatic hernia passes through the greater sciatic foramen into the subgluteal region and can cause sciatica from sciatic nerve compression.
- [Both] Perineal hernia extends through the urogenital diaphragm or between levator ani and coccygeus, often in older women with pelvic surgery, pregnancies, obesity, or ascites.
- [Both] Spermatic cord lipoma/liposarcoma is a fat-containing inguinal canal or scrotal mass mimic. Liposarcoma tends to look expansile or mass-like, may have nonfat soft-tissue components, and can be hyperechoic on ultrasound.
