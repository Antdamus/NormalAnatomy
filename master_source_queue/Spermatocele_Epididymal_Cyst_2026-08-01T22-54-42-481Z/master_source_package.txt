## Source Synthesis Summary

[Both] Spermatocele and epididymal cyst are benign extratesticular cystic lesions of the epididymal region. A spermatocele is a retention cyst of the rete testis or epididymal/efferent duct system that contains spermatozoa, often with internal debris. An epididymal cyst contains simple serous/lymphatic fluid. Imaging often cannot definitively separate the two, and the distinction is usually clinically irrelevant.

[RadPrimer] The canonical teaching frame is ultrasound of the scrotum: a simple or minimally complex cystic mass in the epididymal head, with grayscale ultrasound plus color Doppler. Epididymal head cysts are typically anechoic, thin walled, and avascular. Spermatoceles may contain septations, internal debris, or low-level echoes.

[STATdx] Epididymal cysts may occur anywhere in the epididymis, while spermatoceles classically occur in the epididymal head. STATdx adds that imaging directly over a palpable complaint is important because these complaints often correspond to epididymal cysts.

## Key Facts

- [Both] Typical presentation: painless palpable scrotal mass; cystic masses may transilluminate clinically.
- [Both] Best diagnostic tool: grayscale scrotal ultrasound with color Doppler.
- [Both] Size range: roughly 3 mm to 10 cm.
- [Both] Key benignity clue: cystic extratesticular lesion without vascularized soft tissue is almost always benign and should not be treated as a neoplasm.
- [Both] Falling snow sign: mobile particulate material/low-level echoes within a spermatocele, moved by acoustic radiation force from color Doppler/acoustic streaming.
- [Both] Associated finding: spermatoceles can be associated with dilated rete testis in the mediastinum testis.
- [Both] Treatment: observation if asymptomatic; spermatocelectomy if large enough to cause discomfort.
- [RadPrimer] Aspiration and sclerotherapy with 100% alcohol is noted as a safe and effective treatment technique in selected cases.

## Imaging Findings

### Ultrasound

- [Both] Simple epididymal cyst: anechoic, circumscribed, thin-walled, avascular cystic lesion.
- [Both] Spermatocele: simple or complex cystic mass, sometimes multiseptated, with internal debris or diffuse low-level echoes due to spermatozoa.
- [Both] Falling snow sign: mobile punctate low-level echoes with Doppler-related acoustic streaming, a useful recognition clue for spermatocele.
- [STATdx] Epididymal cysts and spermatoceles are often indistinguishable by imaging; location and internal contents can suggest one term over the other.
- [STATdx] For palpable complaints, the sonographer should scan directly over the area of concern.

### CT and MRI

- [STATdx] CT, when encountered, may show a well-circumscribed uni- or multilocular cystic lesion near the superior pole of the testis.
- [STATdx] MRI shows a cystic lesion that is low on T1, high on T2, and nonenhancing. STATdx image 4 and STATdx image 5 form a paired MR cluster demonstrating nonenhancing septated/T2-bright cystic findings superior to the testis.

## Differential Diagnosis

- [Both] Hematocele: history of trauma; complex fluid in the tunica vaginalis with echogenic clot/fibrin strands. Chronicity can change appearance.
- [Both] Pyocele: epididymoorchitis context with hyperemia/enlarged epididymis or testis; complex tunica vaginalis fluid with debris, septations, low-level echoes, and often a hyperemic rim.
- [Both] Hydrocele: simple or complex fluid within the tunica vaginalis. [STATdx] Hydrocele surrounds the testis, unlike a focal epididymal cyst.
- [Both] Cyst of tunica albuginea: usually small, 2-5 mm, simple cyst on or between layers of the tunica albuginea along the testicular surface.
- [RadPrimer] Abscess: correlate with recent epididymitis and ongoing hyperemia.
- [RadPrimer] Inguinal hernia: should change with Valsalva and show gut signature or echogenic fat.

## Pathology and Mechanism

- [Both] Etiology is often unknown but may reflect scarring after epididymitis, trauma, or vasectomy.
- [STATdx] Spermatoceles may arise from obstruction/dilation of the efferent ductal system.
- [RadPrimer] Grossly, the lesion may contain simple fluid or milky fluid and spermatozoa depending on type.
- [STATdx] Epididymal cysts are epithelial-lined cysts containing serous fluid; spermatoceles can contain spermatozoa, lymphocytes, and debris.

## Clinical Issues and Management

- [Both] These lesions are very common, reported in roughly 20-40% of men, and may occur at any age; STATdx notes they are rarely seen in children.
- [Both] Most are asymptomatic or present as a painless palpable scrotal mass. Rare complications include hypofertility/subfertility or torsion.
- [Both] Management is usually observation if asymptomatic. Surgery is reserved for lesions sufficiently large or symptomatic enough to cause discomfort.
