MASTER SUMMARY
- [Both] Ovarian teratoma includes mature cystic teratoma, immature teratoma, and monodermal teratoma. Mature cystic teratoma is the dermoid cyst and is the common pattern for radiology recognition.
- [Both] The central recognition pattern is an ovarian/adnexal mass containing fat, hair, sebaceous material, or calcified tooth/bone elements. On ultrasound, a shadowing echogenic Rokitansky nodule/dermoid plug is diagnostic when present.
- [Both] Mature cystic teratomas are usually benign and often incidental. They are common ovarian tumors, can be bilateral in 10-20%, may act as a lead point for torsion, and rarely rupture or undergo malignant degeneration.
- [Both] Immature teratoma is a malignant germ cell tumor in younger patients and tends to be large, more solid, irregular, and heterogeneous, with small fat foci and coarse calcification rather than a classic benign dermoid plug.

ULTRASOUND RECOGNITION
- [Both] Ultrasound is the best first-line diagnostic tool. Mature cystic teratoma has a wide range of appearances: heterogeneous cystic mass with echogenic component, highly echogenic fat, fat-fluid level, shadowing mural nodule, tooth-like echogenic foci, hair echoes, and tip-of-iceberg shadowing.
- [Both] Hair can appear as punctate echoes in one plane and linear echoes in an orthogonal plane; mobile hair in fluid can produce the dot-dash-dot appearance.
- [Both] Teeth or calcified elements appear as highly echogenic foci with posterior acoustic shadowing. Fat and sebaceous material can attenuate sound so much that only the leading edge is visible, preventing accurate size measurement.
- [Both] Color Doppler usually shows no flow in the internal dermoid contents. Apparent Doppler signal in calcified components can be twinkling artifact, so pulsed Doppler confirmation is important.
- [RadPrimer] RadPrimer image 1 shows mostly cystic dermoid with floating linear hair echoes and an echogenic shadowing Rokitansky nodule.
- [RadPrimer] RadPrimer image 2 shows a cystic lesion with a fat-containing Rokitansky nodule, reinforcing that mature cystic teratoma can contain tissue from all 3 embryologic germ layers.
- [RadPrimer] RadPrimer image 3 and RadPrimer image 4 show fat-fluid levels, including an endovaginal example where the level is not perfectly horizontal.
- [RadPrimer] RadPrimer image 5 shows an echogenic fat-containing lesion with posterior enhancement from cystic contents and shadowing that obscures deeper structures.
- [RadPrimer] RadPrimer image 6 shows the Doppler rule: dermoid contents usually lack internal flow; flow in a solid component should prompt further CT or MR evaluation for malignant transformation.

CT AND MR RECOGNITION
- [Both] CT diagnosis is straightforward when an adnexal mass contains macroscopic fat. Teeth/calcification are present in a substantial minority of mature cystic teratomas and strongly support the diagnosis.
- [RadPrimer] RadPrimer image 7 shows a CT dermoid with fat-density components and a tooth-like calcification.
- [Both] MR confirms fat by high T1 signal that suppresses on fat-saturated images. Loss of high T1 signal with fat saturation supports macroscopic fat rather than hemorrhagic blood products; out-of-phase signal dropout supports microscopic fat.
- [RadPrimer] RadPrimer images 8-10 form a useful ultrasound-to-MR characterization cluster: a heterogeneous mixed solid-cystic mass without internal flow is further characterized by MR, where T2 and fat-suppressed images confirm macroscopic fat.
- [STATdx] STATdx images 21-23 add a same-patient MR cluster showing T1 hyperintense adnexal fat, signal loss on fat saturation, and T2 context. STATdx image 24 adds another T1 example of high internal fat signal.

COMPLICATIONS AND VARIANTS
- [Both] Rupture is uncommon but can produce low-density fat-containing intraperitoneal fluid and chemical peritonitis with mesenteric stranding or peritoneal surface thickening.
- [Both] Mature cystic teratoma can serve as a lead point for adnexal torsion. Any acute pain presentation should trigger evaluation for ovarian enlargement, abnormal position, twisted pedicle, edema, and abnormal venous/arterial flow.
- [STATdx] STATdx image 15 shows CT edema of intraperitoneal fat adjacent to a mature cystic teratoma in a surgically confirmed torsion case.
- [STATdx] STATdx image 18 shows a transvaginal ultrasound example of torsed teratoma with echogenic plug, cystic component, acute pain, and no demonstrable wall flow.
- [STATdx] STATdx image 17 shows a thick-walled NECT teratoma with surrounding inflammatory change, internal fat, and flecks of calcification.
- [STATdx] STATdx image 26 shows bilateral dermoids in the same patient, reinforcing the 10-20% bilateral rate.
- [Both] Mature cystic teratoma can rarely associate with anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis and paraneoplastic symptoms. Functional monodermal tumors may produce endocrine syndromes: struma ovarii can cause hyperthyroidism, and carcinoid tumor can produce carcinoid syndrome.

IMMATURE AND MONODERMAL TERATOMA
- [Both] Immature teratoma is typically a large mass with irregular enhancing solid components, few cystic areas, small fat foci, coarse or widespread calcification, and hemorrhage. It may have ascites or gliomatosis peritonei.
- [Both] Omental caking or peritoneal nodules in the setting of a fat-containing ovarian mass should raise concern for immature teratoma and glial tissue implantation.
- [Both] Immature teratoma and malignant components may show increased FDG uptake and higher SUVmax than mature cystic teratoma.
- [Both] Struma ovarii is a monodermal teratoma composed predominantly of thyroid tissue. It may present as a cystic lesion with comet-tail artifacts and typically has no significant fat; colloid material can be hypointense on T1WI and T2WI.

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS
- [Both] Hemorrhagic cyst: fine fibrin strands and posterior enhancement favor hemorrhagic cyst, while fat content, shadowing dermoid plug, tooth/calcification, hair echoes, or fat-fluid level favor teratoma.
- [Both] Endometrioma: homogeneous low-level internal echoes, chronic cyclic pain/endometriosis history, and absence of Rokitansky nodule favor endometrioma.
- [Both] Torsion: enlarged ovary with decreased or abnormal flow can coexist with any adnexal mass, including dermoid. Torsion is a complication rather than a mutually exclusive diagnosis.
- [Both] Abscess: pain, fever, inflammatory change, or appendicolith-associated abscess can mimic teratoma. Infarcted dermoid can become infected.
- [Both] Bowel: echogenic feces in large bowel can mimic teratoma. Lack of small-bowel type peristalsis, transvaginal evaluation, repeat exam, or MR can help clarify.

PATHOLOGY
- [Both] Mature cystic teratoma contains mature tissues from endodermal, mesodermal, and ectodermal origin. It is often unilocular, and its fat content is liquid at body temperature but semisolid at room temperature.
- [Both] The Rokitansky nodule protrudes into the cyst cavity; most hair arises from the nodule, and teeth or bone, when present, are typically located in this protuberance.
- [Both] Mature cystic teratoma usually has scant mitotic activity and no cytologic atypia. Malignant degeneration is rare and most often squamous cell carcinoma, with malignant melanoma or sarcoma less common.
- [STATdx] STATdx images 27-29 provide gross pathology correlation for Rokitansky nodule, hair, fat, twisted pedicle, and resected specimen appearance.

CLINICAL COURSE AND MANAGEMENT
- [Both] Mature cystic teratoma is the most common ovarian germ cell tumor and a common excised ovarian neoplasm. It often presents in children and reproductive-age women, with mean mature cystic teratoma presentation around age 30 years.
- [Both] Immature teratoma usually presents from childhood through young adulthood and is a common malignant ovarian germ cell neoplasm.
- [Both] Mature cystic teratomas often grow slowly. Follow-up may be used to record size in selected patients, but definitive treatment is surgical resection when diagnosis, symptoms, size, torsion risk, or complication risk warrants it.
- [Both] Treatment is usually ovarian cystectomy by laparoscopy or laparotomy. Surgical care aims to avoid rupture; if rupture occurs, copious irrigation helps reduce chemical peritonitis.
- [Both] Malignant degeneration risk is higher in older patients, large tumors greater than about 10 cm, and rapidly growing lesions.
