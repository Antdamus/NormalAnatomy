MASTER SUMMARY
- [Both] Functional ovarian cysts are physiologic ovarian cysts of reproductive-age patients, mainly follicles/follicular cysts and corpus luteum/corpus luteum cysts. They are common, usually benign, and most resolve spontaneously.
- [Both] A follicle is a thin-walled anechoic intraovarian cyst under 3 cm in a premenopausal patient. Larger simple anechoic cysts in premenopausal patients are also usually physiologic, but management depends on size and confidence of visualization.
- [Both] A corpus luteum cyst is a thick-walled postovulatory cyst, usually with a hyperechoic wall, possible crenulated margin, central anechoic or hypoechoic cavity, hemorrhage/retracted clot, and marked peripheral Doppler flow.
- [Both] Hemorrhagic functional cysts show lace-like/reticular internal echoes, retracting clot, fluid-fluid or fluid-debris level, and no true internal vascularity; Doppler flow belongs to the wall or surrounding ovarian tissue, not to clot.

ULTRASOUND RECOGNITION
- [Both] Transvaginal ultrasound is the best diagnostic tool. Confirm that the cyst is intraovarian by identifying surrounding ovarian parenchyma, follicles, or bridging ovarian tissue.
- [RadPrimer] Exophytic ovarian cysts can mimic paraovarian or tubal lesions; bridging ovarian parenchyma is a helpful sign that the cyst arises from the ovary.
- [Both] Follicular cysts are thin-walled and anechoic, with no increased internal Doppler flow.
- [Both] Corpus luteum cysts occur after ovulation in the latter part of the menstrual cycle. They are thick-walled, may collapse into a crenulated shape, and may contain internal hemorrhage or retracting clot.
- [Both] The ring-of-fire sign is marked peripheral vascular flow around a corpus luteum cyst wall. It is expected around the normal corpus luteum and is not specific for ectopic pregnancy.
- [STATdx] A very vascular cyst margin can look alarming; do not mistake expected corpus luteum wall vascularity for an enhancing solid component unless vascular solid tissue is truly present.
- [STATdx] Ruptured hemorrhagic cyst can show a hemorrhagic cyst plus moderate pelvic free fluid; free fluid may extend into the upper abdomen.
- [STATdx] Large functional cysts may compress ovarian parenchyma to a thin rim. In a painful enlarged ovary, use pulse Doppler to document arterial and venous waveforms while remembering that Doppler flow does not fully exclude torsion.

CT AND MR
- [Both] CT is often obtained first in nonpregnant patients with acute pain, but it is limited for internal architecture of fluid-attenuation adnexal lesions.
- [Both] On CT, a functional cyst is usually a low-attenuation adnexal mass unless hemorrhagic; hemorrhage may appear as high-attenuation blood products or a fluid-fluid level. Contrast CT may show an enhancing corpus luteum wall corresponding to ring-of-fire vascularity.
- [Both] On MR, follicles are typically T2 hyperintense, T1 hypointense, and nonenhancing. Hemorrhagic cyst signal varies with blood-product age.

DIFFERENTIAL AND PITFALLS
- [Both] Ectopic pregnancy is the key mimic when a ring-of-fire adnexal lesion is present. A corpus luteum cyst is intraovarian and moves with the ovary; a tubal ectopic is usually separate from the ovary and may be displaced away from it with gentle probe pressure.
- [Both] Ectopic pregnancy may have an extraovarian echogenic ring, adnexal hematoma, and echogenic free fluid. Heterotopic pregnancy is a consideration when there is an intrauterine pregnancy plus a separate tubal ectopic, especially after fertility treatment.
- [Both] Ovarian neoplasms in the differential include benign cystic neoplasms such as serous cystadenoma, mucinous cystadenoma, and cystic teratoma; solid tumors such as thecoma-fibroma; and ovarian malignancy.
- [Both] Persistent cystic mass after pregnancy, postmenopausal hemorrhagic-appearing cyst, growth without resolution, or solid vascular components should prompt concern for neoplasm rather than routine functional cyst follow-up.

PATHOLOGY AND NATURAL HISTORY
- [Both] A follicular cyst forms from a persistent follicle when ovulation does not occur and may be associated with elevated estrogen.
- [Both] The corpus luteum forms from the graafian follicle after ovulation. It contains luteinized granulosa cells and a central cystic cavity with fluid/fibrin, often with hemorrhage.
- [Both] If no embryo implants, the corpus luteum degenerates after about 14 days into the corpus albicans. With conception, human chorionic gonadotropin stimulates progesterone production until the placenta assumes progesterone production near the end of the second month.
- [Both] Functional cysts occur during reproductive years. Most are asymptomatic, found incidentally, or found during fertility monitoring. Symptoms may result from capsular distention, rupture/ovulation pain, hemorrhage/hemoperitoneum, or torsion.
- [Both] Corpus luteum cysts may enlarge early in pregnancy, peak around 7 weeks, and usually diminish with pregnancy progression; most are no longer seen by early second trimester.

MANAGEMENT ORIENTATION
- [Both] Malignancy rate in unilocular simple cysts is under 1%, and most functional cysts resolve spontaneously.
- [RadPrimer] SRU 2019 simple cyst guidance for reproductive-age patients: up to 5 cm needs no follow-up; over 5 cm and up to 7 cm can have annual follow-up if confidently characterized, stopping if stable at 2 years; suboptimally evaluated cysts or cysts over 7 cm get short-interval characterization/resolution imaging and later growth-rate evaluation.
- [Both] SRU hemorrhagic cyst guidance for reproductive-age patients: up to 5 cm needs no follow-up; over 5 cm gets follow-up ultrasound in about 6-12 weeks to ensure resolution.
- [STATdx] O-RADS 2022 premenopausal functional cyst guidance: follicles/corpus luteum under 3 cm are O-RADS 1 with no follow-up; simple cysts 3-5 cm are O-RADS 2 with no follow-up; simple cysts over 5 cm and under 10 cm may get 12-month ultrasound follow-up with stopping at 24 months if stable.
- [Both] Classic benign hemorrhagic cysts under 10 cm are O-RADS 2; under 5 cm needs no follow-up, while over 5-10 cm usually gets 8-12 week follow-up and referral/MR if persistent or enlarging.
- [Both] Simple or hemorrhagic cysts over 10 cm are O-RADS 3 and should be managed with follow-up ultrasound and/or MR by gynecology.
- [Both] If a mass decreases in size or resolves, it is benign. Growth without solid components suggests benign neoplasm growth. Solid components, especially with growth, increase malignancy concern.
- [Both] Symptomatic treatment is appropriate for pain. Oral contraceptives may help recurrent cysts. Persistent symptomatic lesions may require laparoscopic fenestration or cystectomy with preservation of normal ovarian tissue.
